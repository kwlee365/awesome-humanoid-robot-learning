## Definition and scope

Humanoid teleoperation covers any system in which a human operator's intent is measured, transported over a communication channel, and reproduced by a humanoid or humanoid-like robot in real time. The scope includes the *interface* (exoskeleton, motion capture, VR headset, camera, GUI), the *retargeting* problem of mapping a human body onto a different kinematic and dynamic embodiment, the *controller* that makes the retargeted reference executable, and the *feedback* returned to the operator. It excludes fully autonomous policies, except where teleoperation generates the demonstrations those policies are trained on — a boundary deliberately blurred since 2023.

## Why this problem matters

Two motivations pull the field in different directions. The first is deployment: a person-driven humanoid is useful today wherever autonomy is not trustworthy — disaster response, remote inspection, and in recent proposals surgical settings such as teleoperated handheld laparoscopy ([LapSurgie](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/lapsurgie-humanoid-robots-performing-surgery-via-teleoperated-handheld-laparosco)). The second is data: humanoid imitation learning needs demonstrations on the robot's own embodiment, and teleoperation is currently the only scalable way to produce them. Deployment rewards fidelity, force feedback and robustness; data collection rewards throughput, portability and low operator training cost. Much of the field's recent structure follows from that tension.

## Historical development

The intellectual foundation is nuclear-industry remote handling. Raymond Goertz's master-slave manipulators at Argonne — for example the 1955-filed [Remote-control manipulator patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2774488A/en) (historical reference, not in the list) — established the still-dominant architecture: a kinematically similar master driven by the human, a slave that follows, and force reflected back. The underlying assumption was *isomorphism*: if master and slave share a kinematic structure, retargeting is trivial and the operator's motor skill transfers directly.

The next decades were about what breaks when the channel is imperfect. Sheridan and Ferrell's study of [remote manipulative control with transmission delay](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1698160/) (historical reference, not in the list) documented the "move-and-wait" strategy operators adopt under delay, motivating supervisory control. Control theory then formalised why delay is dangerous rather than merely annoying: Anderson and Spong's scattering-theory treatment of [bilateral control of teleoperators with time delay](https://doi.org/10.1109/9.24201) and Niemeyer and Slotine's [wave-variable formulation](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1991IJOE...16..152N/abstract) (both historical references, not in the list) showed that a delayed force-feedback loop can be made passive, and therefore stable, at a quantifiable cost in fidelity. Lawrence's [analysis of stability and transparency](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/258054) (historical reference, not in the list) made the trade-off explicit: perfect transparency — the operator feeling the exact remote impedance — and guaranteed stability are in tension. Every later system inherits a position on this curve, usually implicitly.

Humanoids broke the isomorphism assumption. Operator and bipedal robot differ in mass distribution, joint limits, actuator bandwidth and, critically, balance constraints: a pose trivially reachable for the human may be infeasible for the robot. Two responses emerged. One rebuilt isomorphism in hardware through whole-body exoskeleton cockpits — the [TABLIS bilateral exoskeleton cockpit](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9158331/) (historical reference, not in the list) transmits force back to the operator's whole body. The other accepted the embodiment gap and treated retargeting as constrained optimisation.

The DARPA Robotics Challenge era (2013-2015) took a third route, largely abandoning continuous whole-body driving. With bandwidth deliberately degraded, teams converged on supervised autonomy: the operator specified goals, footstep plans and affordances through a GUI, and onboard controllers executed them. Team IHMC's [lessons-learned report](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/rob.21571) (historical reference, not in the list) documents how much of the difficulty lay in interface and operator-workload design rather than control. My reading is that this lesson is under-cited today, though it survives in work on non-expert interfaces such as an [intuitive GUI for non-expert teleoperation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/development-of-an-intuitive-gui-for-non-expert-teleoperation-of-humanoid-robots).

A separate line kept pushing dynamic, continuous coupling. Ramos and Kim's work on [whole-body bilateral feedback teleoperation](https://doi.org/10.1109/TRO.2018.2830387) and on [dynamic locomotion synchronisation between operator and bipedal robot](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scirobotics.aav4282) (both historical references, not in the list) argued that the operator's own balance dynamics can be coupled to the robot's, making the human a physical part of the stabilising loop. That thread continues in wheeled-humanoid work on [heavy lifting via haptic teleoperation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/heavy-lifting-tasks-via-haptic-teleoperation-of-a-wheeled-humanoid) and [whole-body bilateral teleoperation with object parameter estimation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/whole-body-bilateral-teleoperation-with-multi-stage-object-parameter-estimation-), and in [high-speed and impact-resilient teleoperation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/high-speed-and-impact-resilient-teleoperation-of-humanoid-robots).

The decisive shift came around 2023-2024, when learned whole-body controllers replaced model-based tracking as the execution layer. [Learning Human-to-Humanoid Real-Time Whole-Body Teleoperation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-human-to-humanoid-real-time-whole-body-teleoperation) reframed retargeting as training an RL policy to track a cleaned, feasibility-filtered human motion reference, and [OmniH2O](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/omnih2o-universal-and-dexterous-human-to-humanoid-whole-body-teleoperation-and-l) extended this to dexterous, universally-interfaced control. Two assumptions fell at once: the reference need not be dynamically feasible, since the policy absorbs infeasibility, and the interface need not be isomorphic, since the policy consumes sparse keypoints. Cheap interfaces followed — VR headsets with immersive active vision in [Open-TeleVision](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/open-television-teleoperation-with-immersive-active-visual-feedback) and [Bunny-VisionPro](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/bunny-visionpro-real-time-bimanual-dexterous-teleoperation-for-imitation-learnin), passive visual exoskeletons in [ACE](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/ace-a-cross-platform-visual-exoskeletons-system-for-low-cost-dexterous-teleopera), and RGB shadowing in [HumanPlus](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanplus-humanoid-shadowing-and-imitation-from-humans). Sparse-sensor pose estimation from consumer VR, as in [AvatarPoser](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/avatarposer-articulated-full-body-pose-tracking-from-sparse-motion-sensing) and [EgoPoser](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egoposer-robust-real-time-egocentric-pose-estimation-from-sparse-and-intermitten), supplied the missing perception layer.

The second consequence is teleoperation's dual role. Low-cost bimanual puppeteering rigs such as [ALOHA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705) and vision-based dexterous systems like [DexPilot](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03135) and [AnyTeleop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04577) (historical references, not in the list) made the teleoperation system's real output a demonstration dataset. Humanoid work followed: [Deep imitation learning for loco-manipulation through human teleoperation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/deep-imitation-learning-for-humanoid-loco-manipulation-through-human-teleoperati), [3D diffusion policies](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/generalizable-humanoid-manipulation-with-3d-diffusion-policies) and [Mobile-TeleVision](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/mobile-television-predictive-motion-priors-for-humanoid-whole-body-control) all treat the teleoperation stack as a data pipeline. [TWIST2](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/twist2-scalable-portable-and-holistic-humanoid-data-collection-system) states this explicitly, reporting mocap-free VR-based collection of 100 demonstrations in 15 minutes. Evaluation changed accordingly: alongside tracking error, papers now report demonstrations per hour, operator training time and downstream policy success, with simulator-based comparison emerging in [TeleOpBench](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/teleopbench-a-simulator-centric-benchmark-for-dual-arm-dexterous-teleoperation).

## Major technical transitions

- **Isomorphic hardware to learned retargeting.** Kinematic similarity gave way to RL policies tracking sparse keypoints, with feasibility handled in the policy, not the mechanism.
- **Passivity to latency budgeting.** Wave variables addressed unbounded network delay; modern systems assume a local link and minimise pipeline latency, as in [ExtremControl](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/extremcontrol-low-latency-humanoid-teleoperation-with-direct-extremity-control)'s reported 50 ms.
- **Open-loop streaming to closed-loop global tracking.** [CLONE](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/clone-closed-loop-whole-body-humanoid-teleoperation-for-long-horizon-tasks) and [CLOT](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/clot-closed-loop-global-motion-tracking-for-whole-body-humanoid-teleoperation) use localisation feedback to remove long-horizon drift.
- **Full-body retargeting to task-space extremity control.** Some 2026 work maps selected link poses only, trading pose fidelity for latency.
- **Bespoke controllers to pretrained trackers.** [Humanoid-GPT](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-gpt-scaling-data-and-structure-for-zero-shot-motion-tracking) reports a transformer pretrained on a 2B-frame retargeted corpus for zero-shot tracking.

## Representative papers

- [Teleoperation of Humanoid Robots: A Survey](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/teleoperation-of-humanoid-robots-a-survey) — the standard taxonomy of interfaces, retargeting and feedback prior to the RL-tracker era.

- [OmniH2O](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/omnih2o-universal-and-dexterous-human-to-humanoid-whole-body-teleoperation-and-l) — universal interfaces plus dexterous hands on a learned whole-body tracker.

## Current research frontier

Four threads are active. Latency: [ExtremControl](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/extremcontrol-low-latency-humanoid-teleoperation-with-direct-extremity-control) reports 50 ms end-to-end by controlling SE(3) poses of selected extremities instead of the full skeleton. Drift: [CLOT](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/clot-closed-loop-global-motion-tracking-for-whole-body-humanoid-teleoperation) adds high-frequency localisation feedback to an RL tracker trained on roughly 20 hours of human motion. Coverage: [Humanoid-GPT](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-gpt-scaling-data-and-structure-for-zero-shot-motion-tracking) scales a transformer tracker on a 2B-frame corpus, while [TeleGate](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/telegate-whole-body-humanoid-teleoperation-via-gated-expert-selection-with-motio) proposes gated expert selection with a motion prior. Retargeting formulations are also being revisited: a [closed-form geometric solver](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/a-closed-form-geometric-retargeting-solver-for-upper-body-humanoid-robot-teleope) removes iterative IK from the upper-body loop, [stability-aware retargeting](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/stability-aware-retargeting-for-humanoid-multi-contact-teleoperation) reintroduces explicit contact-stability constraints, and [adaptive neural teleoperation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-adaptive-neural-teleoperation-for-humanoid-robots) tunes the mapping online.

## Open problems and unresolved limitations

Force feedback has largely been dropped: the dominant VR and vision interfaces are visually immersive but haptically silent, so the classical transparency question — can the operator feel the remote environment — is unanswered for most modern humanoid systems, and the bilateral line remains a minority position. Evaluation is fragmented; tracking error, demonstration throughput and downstream policy success measure different things, and [TeleOpBench](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/teleopbench-a-simulator-centric-benchmark-for-dual-arm-dexterous-teleoperation) is a first attempt at common ground rather than a settled standard. The embodiment gap persists in subtler form: an RL tracker that silently rejects an infeasible reference gives the operator no signal about *why*, degrading both control and demonstration quality. There is also little systematic evidence on how interface choice biases the resulting datasets. Finally, real network delay — the founding problem of the field — has been mostly assumed away by local-link setups; genuinely remote humanoid teleoperation will have to recover the passivity machinery the learning-based stack currently ignores.
