## Definition and scope

This area covers how a legged robot decides *where* to go and how that decision reaches the walking controller: mapping and localisation for a moving, bobbing sensor; deciding which terrain is safe to step on; choosing waypoints, footsteps or velocity commands; and increasingly taking the goal from an instruction or object category rather than a metric coordinate. It excludes the gait controller itself, though entries such as [Gallant](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/gallant-voxel-grid-based-humanoid-locomotion-and-local-navigation-across-3d-cons) and [Hand-Eye Autonomous Delivery](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/hand-eye-autonomous-delivery-learning-humanoid-navigation-locomotion-and-reachin) train navigation and locomotion together and blur the line. This collection holds only 18 navigation entries, 15 of them from 2025 or later, so what follows is a thin sample rather than a survey.

## Why this problem matters

Navigation is the interface between a humanoid's motor competence and any actual task; a robot that walks well but cannot decide where to walk is a demonstration, not a system. It is also where legged morphology pays off or fails to: stairs, doorsills and clutter are traversable to legs and not to wheels, but only if the navigation layer knows it. Conversely a biped's failure mode is a fall, not a stall, so a decision that is merely inefficient for a wheeled base can be terminal — the framing [STATE-NAV](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/state-nav-stability-aware-traversability-estimation-for-bipedal-navigation-on-ro) makes explicit when it notes bipeds carry greater failure risk than wheeled or quadrupedal platforms.

## Historical development

The inherited architecture came from wheeled robotics and was stable for two decades: build a metric map, localise in it, plan a global path, run a reactive local controller. Its representational core was the occupancy grid of Elfes's "Using Occupancy Grids for Mobile Robot Perception and Navigation" (Computer, 1989; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/2.30720)), mapping and localisation were unified by the probabilistic SLAM formulation surveyed by Durrant-Whyte and Bailey (IEEE RAM, 2006; [publisher page](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1638022/)), and execution fell to velocity-space reactive controllers such as the dynamic window approach of Fox, Burgard and Thrun (IEEE RAM, 1997; [publisher page](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/580977/)) (all historical references, not in the list). The assumptions are worth naming, because everything after is a reaction to them: the robot is a disk sliding on a plane, obstacles are binary and lateral, the configuration space is essentially SE(2), and the map is a static geometric object separable from the controller consuming it.

Legged bodies violate every one. A biped can step *over* a low obstacle and *onto* a raised one, so binary occupancy is the wrong predicate; what matters is whether a surface affords a foothold given the current gait, a property of the robot-terrain pair rather than the terrain. The first humanoid answer kept the architecture but changed the action space from velocities to discrete footsteps: Kuffner et al.'s "Footstep Planning Among Obstacles for Biped Robots" (IROS 2001; [publisher page](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/973406/)) searched a finite set of feasible step transitions, and Chestnutt et al.'s ASIMO planner (2005; [publisher page](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1570188/)) demonstrated it on a commercial humanoid. Localisation changed too, because a walking sensor pitches and rolls and odometry drifts through contact slip; Hornung, Wurm and Bennewitz (IROS 2010; [publisher page](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5649751/)) (all historical references, not in the list) is representative of the move to 3D, multi-level maps. The paradigm was still plan-then-track over a geometric model costly to build and brittle when wrong.

The quadruped literature supplied the pivotal move: replace binary occupancy with a *traversability* score over a robot-centric elevation map. Wermelinger et al.'s "Navigation Planning for Legged Robots in Challenging Terrain" (IROS 2016; [publisher page](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7759199)) planned over slope, roughness and step-height cost layers built on the probabilistic elevation mapping of Fankhauser, Bloesch and Hutter (RA-L 2018; [publisher page](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8392399)) (both historical references, not in the list). The map stopped describing the world and started predicting the robot's own competence in it. Everything since argues about how to compute that prediction.

Two answers still compete. The first learns the prediction but keeps a layered planner. [STATE-NAV](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/state-nav-stability-aware-traversability-estimation-for-bipedal-navigation-on-ro) is the clearest bipedal instance: a transformer predicts instability with uncertainty, traversability becomes the fastest command velocity keeping instability under a chosen limit, and that feeds a sampling-based planner with an MPC tracker. [RL with Data Bootstrapping for Dynamic Subgoal Pursuit](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/rl-with-data-bootstrapping-for-dynamic-subgoal-pursuit-in-humanoid-robot-navigat) keeps the same two-layer shape on Digit but bootstraps the learner from a model-based planner's trajectories.

The second collapses the layers. Rudin et al.'s "Advanced Skills by Learning Locomotion and Local Navigation End-to-End" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.12827)) and Hoeller et al.'s ANYmal Parkour ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14874); [Science Robotics](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scirobotics.adi7566)) (historical references, not in the list) showed one policy taking a goal to joint actions and discovering traversal behaviours no cost function had encoded. The humanoid entries inherit that stance with different perceptual substrates: [Gallant](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/gallant-voxel-grid-based-humanoid-locomotion-and-local-navigation-across-3d-cons) argues depth images and 2.5D elevation maps flatten the world and uses voxelised LiDAR, reporting one policy across overhead constraints, multi-level structures and narrow passages; [ARMOR](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/armor-egocentric-perception-for-humanoid-robot-collision-avoidance-and-motion-pl) distributes wearable-like depth units over the body to fight self-occlusion; [HumanoidPano](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoidpano-hybrid-spherical-panoramic-lidar-cross-modal-perception-for-humanoi) and [Humanoid Occupancy](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-occupancy-enabling-a-generalized-multimodal-occupancy-perception-system) push panoramic and multimodal occupancy shaped around humanoid sensor placement.

A largely separate embodied-AI thread meanwhile changed what a *goal* is. Anderson et al.'s Vision-and-Language Navigation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07280)), the SPL protocol ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06757)) and Habitat ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01201)) normalised instruction-following and object-goal tasks on wheeled or disembodied agents, followed by semantic-map methods such as goal-oriented semantic exploration ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00643)), VLMaps ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05714)) and VLFM ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03275)) (all historical references, not in the list). The bridge to legs arrives with [NaVILA](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/navila-legged-robot-vision-language-action-model-for-navigation), which refuses to map language to joint actions and instead emits mid-level spatial commands in language ("moving forward 75cm") for an RL locomotion policy — a deliberate re-layering after the end-to-end swing. [LOVON](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/lovon-legged-open-vocabulary-object-navigator) pairs an LLM planner with open-vocabulary detection across Go2, B2 and an H1-2 humanoid, and [EgoActor](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egoactor-grounding-task-planning-into-spatial-aware-egocentric-actions-for-human) (2026) predicts locomotion primitives, head motion and manipulation commands from a VLM trained partly on egocentric real demonstrations.

The newest entries add a fourth concern: the sensor's own motion as a first-class variable. [Thinking in 360](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/thinking-in-360-humanoid-visual-search-in-the-wild) makes head rotation in a panorama the learned action and reports proprietary VLMs near 30% success on its benchmark, with path search much harder than object search; [LookOut](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/lookout-real-world-humanoid-egocentric-navigation) predicts egocentric head pose from four hours of Project Aria recordings; [FocusNav](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/focusnav-spatial-selective-attention-with-waypoint-guidance-for-humanoid-local-n) (2026) gates the perceptual field by navigational intent and truncates distant observations when instability is detected. My reading — with 18 entries, a reading rather than a measurement — is that these converge on something the classical stack could not express: perception is an action taken by an unstable body, and the navigation policy must budget it.

## Major technical transitions

Assumptions moved from a planar disk in SE(2) to a stepping body with gait-dependent affordances and overhead constraints. Representations moved from occupancy grids to traversability maps, then split three ways: voxel grids in [Gallant](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/gallant-voxel-grid-based-humanoid-locomotion-and-local-navigation-across-3d-cons), semantic occupancy in [Humanoid Occupancy](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-occupancy-enabling-a-generalized-multimodal-occupancy-perception-system), and no explicit map in policy-based methods. Sensing moved from planar lidar to depth, then panoramic and cross-modal fusion, and in the egocentric line to head-mounted human capture. Architectures oscillated: layered, end-to-end, then layered again with a language interface at the seam ([NaVILA](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/navila-legged-robot-vision-language-action-model-for-navigation)). Data moved from none to simulation at scale — [NavDP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/navdp-learning-sim-to-real-navigation-diffusion-policy-with-privileged-informati) reports over one million metres across 3,000 simulated environments — plus human egocentric video.

## Comparison of dominant approaches

| Approach family | Map requirement | Sensing requirement | Generality to new environments | Coupling to locomotion controller | Typical failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Classical map-plan-execute | Prior or online metric map, explicit | Lidar or depth plus reliable odometry | High within its assumptions, but those are planar | Decoupled; emits a path or velocities | Calls terrain impassable that legs could cross, or the reverse |
| Footstep planning on 2.5D maps | Elevation or plane-segmented map required | Depth or lidar with accurate registration | Moderate; terrain must fit the modelled step set | Tight; plan uses the controller's footstep interface | Search blow-up, and failure on stale or mis-registered maps |
| Traversability-aware hierarchy | Robot-centric local map with learned cost | Depth or lidar plus proprioception | Moderate to high; cost models transfer better than geometry rules | Explicit two layers; subgoals or velocity limits | Miscalibrated risk: over-conservative, or a fall off-distribution |
| End-to-end learned policy | None explicit; state implicit in the network | Egocentric RGB or depth, often only that | Strong in-distribution, unknown outside it | Fused, or trained jointly with the gait policy | Silent out-of-distribution failure with no diagnosable intermediate |
| Language- or object-conditioned | None, or a semantic map built on the fly | RGB plus a pretrained vision-language model | Best for goal specification, weakest for metric precision | Loose; high- or mid-level commands to a locomotion policy | Grounding errors, hallucinated targets, latency against a gait cycle |

## Representative papers

- [NoMaD](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/nomad-goal-masked-diffusion-policies-for-navigation-and-exploration) (2023) — one diffusion policy covering goal-directed navigation and undirected exploration via goal masking.
- [NaVILA](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/navila-legged-robot-vision-language-action-model-for-navigation) (2024) — legged VLA emitting mid-level spatial commands in language instead of joint actions.
- [ARMOR](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/armor-egocentric-perception-for-humanoid-robot-collision-avoidance-and-motion-pl) (2024) — distributed wearable-style depth sensing on a GR1 humanoid with a transformer planner trained on human motion.
- [HumanoidPano](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoidpano-hybrid-spherical-panoramic-lidar-cross-modal-perception-for-humanoi) (2025) — panoramic-vision and LiDAR fusion producing BEV segmentation for humanoid navigation.
- [NavDP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/navdp-learning-sim-to-real-navigation-diffusion-policy-with-privileged-informati) (2025) — sim-only diffusion policy with a trajectory critic trained under privileged information, transferred without fine-tuning.
- [LOVON](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/lovon-legged-open-vocabulary-object-navigator) (2025) — LLM planning plus open-vocabulary detection for object goals across quadrupeds and a humanoid.
- [Humanoid Occupancy](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-occupancy-enabling-a-generalized-multimodal-occupancy-perception-system) (2025) — multimodal semantic occupancy with a sensor-placement study specific to humanoid kinematic interference.
- [STATE-NAV](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/state-nav-stability-aware-traversability-estimation-for-bipedal-navigation-on-ro) (2025) — learned instability prediction with uncertainty as the definition of bipedal traversability.
- [Gallant](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/gallant-voxel-grid-based-humanoid-locomotion-and-local-navigation-across-3d-cons) (2025) — voxelised LiDAR as the interface for joint locomotion and local navigation under genuinely 3D constraints.
- [Thinking in 360](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/thinking-in-360-humanoid-visual-search-in-the-wild) (2025) — makes where to look the learned action, with a benchmark current VLMs score poorly on.

## Current research frontier

Four threads are visibly active. Perceptual representation is unsettled: voxels, panoramic BEV, semantic occupancy and no-map policies are argued for simultaneously with no shared benchmark to adjudicate. Risk-aware traversability for bipeds is newly learnable rather than hand-tuned. Goal specification is moving to open vocabulary and instructions, with [EgoActor](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egoactor-grounding-task-planning-into-spatial-aware-egocentric-actions-for-human) extending that from waypoints to locomotion primitives, head motion and manipulation in one action space. And active perception — [FocusNav](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/focusnav-spatial-selective-attention-with-waypoint-guidance-for-humanoid-local-n) coupling attention to stability, [LookOut](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/lookout-real-world-humanoid-egocentric-navigation) learning head pose from human data — treats gaze as part of the policy. Three entries sit outside these threads and are best read as isolated probes: [Learning Social Navigation from Positive and Negative Demonstrations](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-social-navigation-from-positive-and-negative-demonstrations-and-rule-ba), [Quantum Deep RL for humanoid navigation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/quantum-deep-reinforcement-learning-for-humanoid-robot-navigation-task), which reports simulated MuJoCo results only, and [INTENTION](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/intention-inferring-tendencies-of-humanoid-robot-motion-through-interactive-intu), which is closer to interaction memory than to navigation proper.

## Open problems and unresolved limitations

Evaluation is the largest gap. The embodied-AI community converged on shared tasks and SPL ([Anderson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06757), historical reference, not in the list); the humanoid entries here mostly report single-platform demonstrations on bespoke courses, so generality claims cannot be compared across papers. Second, traversability is defined per paper, and only [STATE-NAV](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/state-nav-stability-aware-traversability-estimation-for-bipedal-navigation-on-ro) ties it to a calibrated instability prediction with uncertainty; otherwise "safe" is a threshold someone chose. Third, the layered-versus-end-to-end question may be miscast — [NaVILA](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/navila-legged-robot-vision-language-action-model-for-navigation) suggests what matters is what the interface between layers carries, not how many layers exist. Fourth, VLM-based grounding runs far slower than a gait cycle, and nothing here resolves how a stale or hallucinated target is caught before the robot commits a step. Fifth, long-horizon state is weak: most learned policies here are reactive over a short observation history, with no persistent map for backtracking or recovery after localisation loss. Finally, with 18 entries, three predating 2025 and several only tangentially about navigation, this page documents an area that is forming — the apparent convergence on egocentric, language-conditioned, stability-aware navigation may partly reflect which papers were collected.
