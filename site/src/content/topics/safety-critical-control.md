## Definition and scope

This area covers control machinery whose object is constraint satisfaction rather than task performance: mechanisms that take a nominal command — from an operator, a planner, a whole-body controller or a learned policy — and decide whether executing it will leave a set the robot must stay inside. Control barrier functions and the quadratic-program safety filters built on them are the centre of gravity, with safety-constrained model predictive control, whole-body inverse dynamics under barrier constraints, constrained reinforcement learning, self-collision avoidance and the certification argument arranged around them. The nominal controller is not the object of study: a locomotion policy that happens not to fall belongs to the locomotion area, while a filter that provably rules out a class of joint-limit or collision violations belongs here. Boundaries are porous — [SHIELD: Safety on Humanoids via CBFs In Expectation on Learned Dynamics](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/shield-safety-on-humanoids-via-cbfs-in-expectation-on-learned-dynamics) and [Geometry-Aware Predictive Safety Filters on Humanoids](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/geometry-aware-predictive-safety-filters-on-humanoids) also appear in the locomotion discussion, and [Whole-Body Planning for Humanoids Navigating Confined Spaces via Self-Collision Avoidance References](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/whole-body-planning-for-humanoids-navigating-confined-spaces-via-self-collision-) is as much a planning paper as a safety one.

One thread sits awkwardly under the same heading. Fall safety and protective control start from the opposite premise to set invariance: they assume the invariance argument has already failed and ask what to do in the fraction of a second that remains. Entries such as [SafeFall: Learning Protective Control for Humanoid Robots](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safefall-learning-protective-control-for-humanoid-robots) share almost no vocabulary with the barrier literature, and grouping them is a curatorial choice rather than a settled one. The 21 entries are also skewed in time — three predate 2022, thirteen are from 2025 or 2026 — so the theoretical apparatus the recent work depends on appears here only as external references, marked as such.

## Why this problem matters

The sharpest statement of why humanoids are a special case comes from the certification side. [Toward Certified Functional Safety for Industrial Humanoid Robots: The Fail-Passive Gap and a Feasibility Study](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/toward-certified-functional-safety-for-industrial-humanoid-robots-the-fail-passi) observes that industrial machine safety rests on a fail-passive assumption — the safe state is the de-energised state, reached by removing power — which is built into the design methodology of ISO 13849-1 and its electrical companion EN 60204-1 (ISO 13849-1:2023, [ISO catalogue entry](https://www.iso.org/standard/73481.html); historical reference, not in the list). A walking biped inverts this. Its safe state is actively controlled, so cutting power produces an uncontrolled fall of a machine weighing tens of kilograms, and every conventional stop category becomes a hazard rather than a mitigation. The paper's response is a certified external safety chain wrapped around an uncertified robot, validated on a Unitree G1 EDU pick-and-place cell in a semi-enclosed workspace.

Underneath that sits a technical problem with four components. Legged systems are hybrid: the impact map at touchdown can carry the state out of a set that was invariant throughout the preceding continuous phase, so continuous-time invariance arguments do not compose across a gait cycle without extra work. They are underactuated: the six floating-base degrees of freedom have no direct actuator, so a barrier written on base or centre-of-mass state has relative degree greater than one and the naive barrier condition contains no control input at all. They are high-dimensional, which puts hand-designed barriers and exact reachability computations equally out of reach at thirty degrees of freedom. And their models are worst precisely where safety is decided — contact, friction, actuator dynamics, compliance — so a guarantee conditioned on an accurate model is conditioned on the part of the model one trusts least. What is new is that the nominal controller is now usually a neural network making no statement about constraints at all, which forces safety into a separate, explicitly reasoned-about layer.

## Historical development

The base technique is older than its robotics application and simpler than its reputation. A barrier certificate proves a set is never left; the move that made it a design tool was to require not that the set be invariant under fixed dynamics but that a control input exist which keeps it invariant, formalised in Wieland and Allgöwer's "Constructive Safety Using Control Barrier Functions" (IFAC NOLCOS 2007; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3182/20070822-3-ZA-2920.00076)) (historical reference, not in the list). Ames, Xu, Grizzle and Tabuada ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06408)) supplied the form everything downstream uses: for a control-affine system, requiring the barrier's derivative to exceed a class-K function of the barrier itself is an inequality affine in the input, so it drops into a quadratic program that returns the admissible input closest to the nominal command, with the theory consolidated in a later overview ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11199)) (both historical references, not in the list). Three properties explain why it spread, and the rest of the field is a negotiation with them: the program is convex and pointwise in time, so it runs inside a kilohertz loop; it is minimally invasive, doing nothing when the command is already safe; and its guarantee is forward invariance of the barrier's zero-superlevel set, conditional on an exact model, on feasibility at every instant, and on the state being known.

Bipeds were an early target and an early source of trouble. Hsu, Xu and Ames combined barrier and control Lyapunov constraints in one program for bipedal walking (ACC 2015; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACC.2015.7172044)), exposing the relative-degree problem: for a position-level constraint on a mechanical system the input does not appear until the second derivative. Exponential control barrier functions (Nguyen and Sreenath, ACC 2016; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACC.2016.7524935)) and the high-relative-degree generalisation (Xiao and Belta, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04706)) resolved it by differentiating until the input surfaces and constraining the resulting chain — at the cost of a pole-placement choice that sets how aggressively the filter intervenes and has no principled setting. The canonical demonstration became foot placement on stepping stones (Nguyen, Hereid, Grizzle, Ames and Sreenath, CDC 2016; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/CDC.2016.7798370)) (all historical references, not in the list), a constraint enforced at a discrete hybrid transition rather than continuously, which is why that task persists as a benchmark long after the method changed.

Dimensionality got a structural answer borrowed from the walking literature. Hybrid zero dynamics (Westervelt, Grizzle and Koditschek, IEEE TAC 2003; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2002.806653)) (historical reference, not in the list) had established that zeroing a well-chosen output leaves a low-dimensional invariant surface carrying the underactuated behaviour. [Neural Gaits: Learning Bipedal Locomotion via Control Barrier Functions and Zero Dynamics Policies](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/neural-gaits-learning-bipedal-locomotion-via-control-barrier-functions-and-zero-) takes that surface as the barrier's domain, posing walking as set invariance on the reduced-order zero dynamics rather than as tracking or reward maximisation, which shrinks the learning problem sharply while the barrier machinery is claimed to lift the guarantee back to the full-order system, with episodic refinement from robot data and a demonstration on an underactuated biped. Model error got its answer in parallel: [Robust Safety-Critical Control for Dynamic Robotics](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robust-safety-critical-control-for-dynamic-robotics) puts robust control Lyapunov functions and robust barrier functions into a single program carrying input constraints, so the guarantee is stated against a bounded uncertainty set. That entry needs a caveat the list metadata does not carry: its bipedal results are simulations of a five-link planar walker and the hardware validation is a rectilinear spring-cart — a recurring pattern in which the theory is demonstrated on the system where its assumptions hold.

Humanoids then pushed the constraint set inward, from the environment to the robot's own body. [Humanoid Self-Collision Avoidance Using Whole-Body Control with Control Barrier Functions](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-self-collision-avoidance-using-whole-body-control-with-control-barrier-) argues that reactive self-collision schemes cannot guarantee collision-free trajectories because they reason kinematically and ignore the underactuated dynamics, and integrates barriers into the whole-body controller on the MIT Humanoid — in simulation only. [Safe Whole-Body Task Space Control for Humanoid Robots](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safe-whole-body-task-space-control-for-humanoid-robots) carries the idea to hardware on Agility Robotics' Digit with acceleration-based exponential barriers inside a quadratic-program inverse-dynamics controller, and is notable for what it constrains: not obstacles but the failure modes of naively stacking whole-body tasks — stepping on the support foot, leaning past a recoverable torso angle, generating centroidal momentum the next contact cannot absorb. Carry enough barriers and they contradict each other, which [Safety-critical Locomotion of Biped Robots in Infeasible Paths: Overcoming Obstacles during Navigation toward Destination](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safety-critical-locomotion-of-biped-robots-in-infeasible-paths-overcoming-obstac) confronts by using outcomes of physical interactions with unknown objects to rank competing conditions and generate a reference under that hierarchy. My reading is that this entry matters more than its visibility suggests, because it concedes what the framework usually assumes away: the intersection of the safe sets can be empty, and when it is, something must decide which constraint to break.

Where the barrier comes from changed next, because hand design does not scale past a few analytic constraints. One substitute is learning the certificate — barrier functions fit to expert demonstrations (Robey and colleagues, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03315)), or the model residual entering the barrier condition learned instead of the barrier itself (Taylor, Singletary, Yue and Ames, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10099)), with the space of neural Lyapunov, barrier and contraction certificates and their shared verification gap mapped by Dawson, Gao and Fan ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11762)) (all historical references, not in the list). The other is geometric construction from perception: solving a Dirichlet problem for Poisson's equation over observed free space yields a function whose superlevel set is the safe set, so the barrier is computed from sensor data rather than written by a designer ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06794)) (historical reference, not in the list). [Geometry-Aware Predictive Safety Filters on Humanoids](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/geometry-aware-predictive-safety-filters-on-humanoids) extends this in the two directions humanoids need, reformulating the static Dirichlet problem as a parameterised moving-boundary problem so the safe set tracks a changing scene, and applying Minkowski set operations to lift the domain into a configuration space that accounts for the robot's own geometry — encoding the humanoid-specific fact that a legged robot with arms is an asymmetric manipulable volume, not the disk that mobile-robot collision avoidance assumes.

Folding barriers into a prediction horizon addresses the pointwise program's other weakness, myopia: satisfying the condition now can steer into states where no admissible input satisfies it later, since persistent feasibility is an assumption rather than a result. Wabersich and Zeilinger make the certificate a trajectory-level object by requiring a feasible backup plan over a horizon ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05506)), Zeng, Zhang and Sreenath embed discrete-time barrier conditions in the MPC constraint set ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11718)), and Grandia, Taylor, Ames and Hutter demonstrate a layered version on ANYmal with barriers in both a low-frequency kino-dynamic MPC and a high-frequency tracking controller ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00032)) (all historical references, not in the list). The geometry-aware filter is the humanoid instance of that line. A related move pushes safety further upstream into planning: [Whole-Body Planning for Humanoids Navigating Confined Spaces via Self-Collision Avoidance References](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/whole-body-planning-for-humanoids-navigating-confined-spaces-via-self-collision-) puts differentiable collision avoidance inside a reachability-constrained formulation over rigid-body volumes, uses the result to seed a full-order trajectory optimiser, and trains a residual reinforcement-learning policy to track the plan on a Unitree G1 through confined testbeds. Here safety is a property of the reference and the runtime layer only has to follow it.

The collision with reinforcement learning is the defining event of the last two years, and it happened at two points in the pipeline. The runtime placement wraps a trained policy. [**SPARK**: A Toolbox for Safe Humanoid Autonomy and Teleoperation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/spark-a-toolbox-for-safe-humanoid-autonomy-and-teleoperation) packages modular safe-control algorithms with configurable criteria and sensitivity plus comparative simulation benchmarks, making it the closest thing the area has to shared infrastructure. [SHIELD: Safety on Humanoids via CBFs In Expectation on Learned Dynamics](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/shield-safety-on-humanoids-via-cbfs-in-expectation-on-learned-dynamics) attacks the model-accuracy prerequisite by fitting a generative stochastic residual dynamics model to hardware rollouts of the nominal controller and enforcing a discrete-time barrier condition in probability — trading a deterministic guarantee for a probabilistic one that is at least about the deployed system. [Safe Execution of RL Policies via Acceleration-based CBF-QP Constraint Enforcement for Real-World Robotic Deployments](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safe-execution-of-rl-policies-via-acceleration-based-cbf-qp-constraint-enforceme) enforces joint position, velocity, torque and collision limits in one acceleration-level program requiring no retraining, on a Kinova Gen3 manipulator and a 19-degree-of-freedom Unitree H1; its more useful contribution is arguably the task formulation, since defining what to minimise when the filter must deviate — torque deviation in one variant, induced acceleration deviation in another — turns the safety-performance trade-off into an explicit design parameter rather than an artefact of objective weighting.

The training-time placement exists because the runtime one has a known pathology: the policy does not know the filter is there, keeps proposing actions the filter keeps rejecting, and the composite is more conservative and less competent than either part. [CBF-RL: Safety Filtering Reinforcement Learning in Training with Control Barrier Functions](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/cbf-rl-safety-filtering-reinforcement-learning-in-training-with-control-barrier-) moves the filter into training — modifying the nominal policy with a barrier term and filtering rollouts as they are collected, with a result showing the continuous-time filter can be applied to discrete-time rollouts in closed form — and reports obstacle avoidance and stair climbing on a Unitree G1 with no runtime filter at all. [End-to-End Humanoid Robot Safe and Comfortable Locomotion Policy](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/end-to-end-humanoid-robot-safe-and-comfortable-locomotion-policy) takes the constrained-MDP route, translating barrier conditions into costs enforced by penalised proximal policy optimisation while mapping raw spatio-temporal lidar to motor commands, and adds comfort-oriented rewards drawn from human-robot interaction research. Both are the legged instance of an older argument between constrained policy optimisation (Achiam, Held, Tamar and Abbeel, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10528)) and barrier-guided reinforcement learning (Cheng, Orosz, Murray and Burdick, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08792)) (both historical references, not in the list). Neither placement dominates: moving the constraint into training buys competence and loses the ability to edit the constraint set without retraining, which was the property that made runtime filters attractive in the first place.

[PAC-MAN: Perception-Aware CBF-RL for Whole-Body Safety in Humanoid Dodgeball](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/pac-man-perception-aware-cbf-rl-for-whole-body-safety-in-humanoid-dodgeball) takes the state-estimation dependency seriously, using barrier guidance over clearance to every body link during training while the deployed policy sees only segmentation-masked depth from a head-mounted camera, with zero-shot deployment on a Unitree G1 succeeding on 95 percent of throws. Its central finding is one the area has mostly avoided stating: which barrier structure is usable depends on perceptual observability, the finer joint-level barrier performing best with accurate ball states and degrading under fixed-camera observation until a tracking gimbal or privileged filter is restored. A barrier is a function of the state, and the guarantee inherits every deficiency of the estimate.

A quieter thread treats the human as the hazard. In teleoperation and imitation the dangerous input is the command rather than the environment, because morphologies differ and a retargeted reference routinely asks for a self-collision, a joint-limit violation or a singular configuration. [Safe Human-to-Humanoid Motion Imitation Using Control Barrier Functions](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safe-human-to-humanoid-motion-imitation-using-control-barrier-functions) makes this explicit, extracting skeletal keypoints from a single camera, retargeting them, and passing the result through a barrier-function program rather than learning safety implicitly — in simulation. [MIRROR: Visual Motion Imitation via Real-time Retargeting and TeleOperation with Parallel Differential Inverse Kinematics](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/mirror-visual-motion-imitation-via-real-time-retargeting-and-teleoperation-with-) targets the failure where differential inverse kinematics stalls near joint limits, singularities and active self-collision boundaries, evaluating many constrained IK programs in parallel on a GPU with a self-collision barrier in each and a Lyapunov-based selection criterion, on the THEMIS humanoid. Treating the barrier as something you can afford to evaluate hundreds of times per tick is a compute-driven change in what the formulation is good for, not a change in the formulation.

The fall thread runs on a different premise and, in my reading, a different objective function. Its prehistory is humanoid work on controlled descent and damage reduction, including direction-changing fall control that reorients a falling robot away from obstacles (Goswami and colleagues, Autonomous Robots 2014; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10514-013-9343-2)), surveyed in a recent review of fall coping strategies ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42235-024-00643-y)) (both historical references, not in the list). The concept linking it to the invariance literature is capturability — when a fall is still avoidable by stepping (Koolen and colleagues, IJRR 2012; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364912452673)) (historical reference, not in the list) — because that is precisely the trigger a protective controller needs. [Robust Humanoid Contact Planning with Learned Zero- and One-Step Capturability Prediction](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robust-humanoid-contact-planning-with-learned-zero-and-one-step-capturability-pr) is the bridge, learning capturability prediction to score candidate contact sequences in a search-based footstep planner on the observation that a recovery controller fails whenever the robot cannot reach a contact able to reject the disturbance.

The 2025-2026 cluster then abandons avoidance as the sole objective. [SafeFall: Learning Protective Control for Humanoid Robots](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safefall-learning-protective-control-for-humanoid-robots) pairs a GRU fall predictor with a policy that stays dormant until a fall is judged unavoidable and is trained against a damage-aware reward keyed to the robot's structural vulnerabilities, reporting on a full-scale Unitree G1 a 68.3 percent reduction in peak contact forces, a 78.4 percent reduction in peak joint torques, and elimination of 99.3 percent of collisions with vulnerable components. [Unified Humanoid Fall-Safety Policy from a Few Demonstrations](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/unified-humanoid-fall-safety-policy-from-a-few-demonstrations) argues against treating prevention, controlled descent and stand-up as three problems, fusing sparse human demonstrations with reinforcement learning and an adaptive diffusion-based memory of safe reactions into one policy. [VIGOR: Visual Goal-In-Context Inference for Unified Humanoid Fall Safety](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/vigor-visual-goal-in-context-inference-for-unified-humanoid-fall-safety) makes the same unification argument and adds terrain, distilling a privileged teacher into a student running on egocentric depth and proprioception that matches the teacher's goal-in-context latent — the next target pose fused with local terrain rather than perception and action encoded separately. [Embedding Classical Balance Control Principles in Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Recovery](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/embedding-classical-balance-control-principles-in-reinforcement-learning-for-hum) comes at recovery from the classical side, supplying capture point, centre-of-mass state and centroidal momentum as privileged critic inputs and reward-shaping terms while the actor uses proprioception alone, reporting a 93.4 percent recovery rate on a Unitree H1-2 and, more informatively, an ablation in which removing the balance structure makes stand-up learning fail outright. That ablation is the strongest evidence here that the classical reduced-order quantities carry real information rather than decorating a reward.

## Major technical transitions

What the barrier is written on moved from a handful of analytic scalar constraints — a foot-placement window, a joint limit, a distance to one obstacle — through robot-geometry-aware configuration-space sets, to functions synthesised online from perception or learned from demonstrations. Where the constraint is imposed moved outward from a pointwise program at the torque or acceleration level in both directions: down into whole-body inverse dynamics, up into a receding horizon, up again into contact and trajectory planning, and sideways into the reinforcement-learning objective itself. What the guarantee is conditioned on moved from an exact model, through a bounded uncertainty set, to a learned stochastic residual model yielding a statement in probability. The relationship between safety layer and nominal controller moved from correcting a model-based controller that shared its assumptions, to correcting a neural policy that shares none of them, to training that policy against the constraint so no correction is needed. Evaluation moved from planar bipeds in simulation to full-scale commercial humanoids — Unitree G1 and H1, Digit, THEMIS — and, in one entry, to a certified safety chain assessed against a machinery standard rather than a trajectory. And the objective split: half the area still tries to prove the robot never leaves a set, while the other half measures peak contact force, peak joint torque and which body part hit the ground.

| Predictive / CBF-constrained MPC ([geometry-aware filters](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/geometry-aware-predictive-safety-filters-on-humanoids), [confined-space planning](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/whole-body-planning-for-humanoids-navigating-confined-spaces-via-self-collision-)) | A usable predictive model over the horizon and reliable perception | Over a receding horizon, or in the reference itself | Recursive feasibility, given the horizon assumptions hold | Highest; a nonlinear program at reduced rate | Horizon too short for the disturbance; stale perception poisons the safe set |

| Constraint-in-training RL ([CBF-RL](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/cbf-rl-safety-filtering-reinforcement-learning-in-training-with-control-barrier-), [CMDP locomotion](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/end-to-end-humanoid-robot-safe-and-comfortable-locomotion-policy), [PAC-MAN](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/pac-man-perception-aware-cbf-rl-for-whole-body-safety-in-humanoid-dodgeball)) | The simulator covers the states where constraints bind | In the learning objective and the training rollouts | Empirical violation rates; no runtime certificate | Training cost only; deployment is a forward pass | Changing a constraint requires retraining; violations off-distribution |
| Fall and protective control ([SafeFall](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safefall-learning-protective-control-for-humanoid-robots), [unified fall-safety](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/unified-humanoid-fall-safety-policy-from-a-few-demonstrations), [VIGOR](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/vigor-visual-goal-in-context-inference-for-unified-humanoid-fall-safety)) | A fall predictor that fires early enough and rarely enough | After invariance has already been given up | None; a measured reduction in impact force, torque and component contact | Low; a dormant policy plus a small predictor | False negatives leave no time to act; false positives abort recoverable states |

## Representative papers

- [Robust Safety-Critical Control for Dynamic Robotics](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robust-safety-critical-control-for-dynamic-robotics) — robust CLFs and CBFs in one program with input constraints; the model-uncertainty statement of the problem.

- [Safe Whole-Body Task Space Control for Humanoid Robots](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safe-whole-body-task-space-control-for-humanoid-robots) — exponential barriers inside whole-body inverse dynamics, on Digit hardware.
- [Safety-critical Locomotion of Biped Robots in Infeasible Paths: Overcoming Obstacles during Navigation toward Destination](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safety-critical-locomotion-of-biped-robots-in-infeasible-paths-overcoming-obstac) — what to do when the safe sets do not intersect.

## Current research frontier

Four directions are visible in the 2026 entries. The first is coupling the barrier to real onboard perception rather than a privileged state, which [PAC-MAN](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/pac-man-perception-aware-cbf-rl-for-whole-body-safety-in-humanoid-dodgeball) treats as the central variable and [Geometry-Aware Predictive Safety Filters on Humanoids](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/geometry-aware-predictive-safety-filters-on-humanoids) approaches from the set-synthesis side; the shared open question is what a guarantee means when the safe set is recomputed every frame from a noisy depth image. The second is unification of the runtime layer — [Acc-CBF-QP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safe-execution-of-rl-policies-via-acceleration-based-cbf-qp-constraint-enforceme) putting joint, torque and collision limits in one program across a manipulator and a humanoid, and [SPARK](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/spark-a-toolbox-for-safe-humanoid-autonomy-and-teleoperation) trying to make the choice among filters a configuration rather than a research project. The third is consolidation of fall safety into single policies spanning prevention through recovery, where [Unified Humanoid Fall-Safety Policy from a Few Demonstrations](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/unified-humanoid-fall-safety-policy-from-a-few-demonstrations) and [VIGOR](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/vigor-visual-goal-in-context-inference-for-unified-humanoid-fall-safety) make the same architectural argument from different data. The fourth is certification. [Toward Certified Functional Safety for Industrial Humanoid Robots](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/toward-certified-functional-safety-for-industrial-humanoid-robots-the-fail-passi) is the only entry here whose deliverable is a regulatory artefact rather than a controller, and it reframes the rest: a barrier-function argument, however sound, is not a safety-integrity-level case, and none of the methods on this page has been through that process.

## Open problems and unresolved limitations

The guarantees rest on an assumption stack that hardware violates at every layer, and the layers are worth naming rather than gesturing at. The model must be accurate, and it is least accurate at contact. Disturbances must be bounded, and the bound is chosen by the designer. The program must stay feasible at every instant, which pointwise formulations cannot promise and predictive ones promise only within a horizon and a model. The state must be known, and [PAC-MAN](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/pac-man-perception-aware-cbf-rl-for-whole-body-safety-in-humanoid-dodgeball) shows the guarantee degrading with observability rather than failing loudly. Actuators must deliver what the program requests, which torque limits and saturation routinely deny. Each is acknowledged somewhere in the literature; almost nothing reports what happens when several fail at once, which is the ordinary condition of a humanoid on unmodelled terrain.

The evidence base is uneven in a way the list metadata makes checkable. Four of the 21 entries record no hardware result at all, and a fifth, [Robust Safety-Critical Control for Dynamic Robotics](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robust-safety-critical-control-for-dynamic-robotics), validates on a linear cart while its bipedal claims are simulated. Where hardware exists it is overwhelmingly one machine — the Unitree G1 appears in a majority of the 2025-2026 entries — so cross-embodiment generality is asserted rather than shown, and results are demonstration counts and success rates on tasks each paper defines for itself. [**SPARK**](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/spark-a-toolbox-for-safe-humanoid-autonomy-and-teleoperation) is the only entry offering comparative benchmarks across safety approaches, and those are in simulation.

Conservatism is the cost nobody quantifies. The stated advantage of a minimally-invasive filter is that it does nothing when the command is already safe, yet the measured quantity is almost always the reduction in violations, not the task performance surrendered to obtain it. [CBF-RL](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/cbf-rl-safety-filtering-reinforcement-learning-in-training-with-control-barrier-) and the deviation objective in [Acc-CBF-QP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safe-execution-of-rl-policies-via-acceleration-based-cbf-qp-constraint-enforceme) both target this, but there is no agreed metric for how much capability a safety layer costs, and without one the training-time and runtime placements cannot be compared on the axis that actually distinguishes them.

The two halves of the area barely communicate. Invariance work reports whether a set was left; protective work reports peak contact force, peak joint torque and which component struck the ground. There is no shared quantity, no common benchmark, and no formulation in this list that treats residual risk and the damage integral as terms in one objective — even though capturability is the obvious interface and [Robust Humanoid Contact Planning with Learned Zero- and One-Step Capturability Prediction](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robust-humanoid-contact-planning-with-learned-zero-and-one-step-capturability-pr) already computes something close to it. Fall predictors also carry an asymmetric error cost that goes unexamined: a false negative leaves no time to protect, a false positive aborts a state the nominal controller could have recovered, and the operating points chosen are not justified against that asymmetry. Finally, the certification gap named by [Toward Certified Functional Safety for Industrial Humanoid Robots](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/toward-certified-functional-safety-for-industrial-humanoid-robots-the-fail-passi) is not a formality that better control theory dissolves: the standards assume a de-energised safe state, a legged robot has none, and the interim answer of a certified external chain confines the robot to a fenced cell — the deployment scenario humanoids were meant to escape. My reading is that this, rather than any open question in barrier synthesis, is what currently limits the area's practical reach.
