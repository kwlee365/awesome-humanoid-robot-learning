## Definition and scope

This area covers the methods that make a policy trained in a physics simulator work on physical hardware: randomising what the simulator gets wrong, identifying what it could get right, learning an adaptation signal at run time, correcting the residual with real data, and fine-tuning on the robot itself. It also covers how transfer is *measured* — sim-to-sim validation, out-of-distribution detection, failure diagnosis.

A caveat worth stating plainly: only 13 entries in this collection are filed here, while sim-to-real technique is embedded in most papers in every other area. Almost any humanoid locomotion or whole-body result since 2023 contains a randomisation table, an actuator model, or a teacher-student stage. The papers filed here are those whose *primary contribution* is the transfer mechanism. For the technique as applied, see [locomotion](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/topics/locomotion) and [loco-manipulation and whole-body control](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/topics/loco-manipulation-and-whole-body-control).

## Why this problem matters

Simulated humanoid experience is effectively free and physical experience is not: hardware breaks, resets need a human, and a falling biped is a safety event. Every practical humanoid pipeline therefore trains where data is cheap and deploys where it is expensive, and the whole enterprise rests on whether that transfer holds. The gap is not one thing. It is actuator dynamics and friction that no rigid-body model captures, contact and impact models that are numerical conveniences rather than physics, communication and control latency, sensor noise and drift, unmodelled compliance in links and drives, and inertial parameters that were never measured. These sources differ in whether they are *identifiable*, *randomisable*, or *only correctable from real data* — which is why the field produced several distinct method families rather than converging on one.

## Historical development

The first generation treated the gap as an unknown to be covered rather than measured. Tobin et al., "Domain Randomization for Transferring Deep Neural Networks from Simulation to the Real World" (2017; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06907)) (historical reference, not in the list) randomised textures, lighting and camera pose so reality would look like another sample from the training distribution; Peng et al., "Sim-to-Real Transfer of Robotic Control with Dynamics Randomization" (2017; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06537)) (historical reference, not in the list) moved the idea from pixels to physics parameters, and OpenAI's "Learning Dexterous In-Hand Manipulation" (2018; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00177)) (historical reference, not in the list) scaled it to a contact-rich task by randomising friction and appearance. The implicit assumption was that the true system lies inside the randomised support; the implicit cost was conservativeness, since a policy robust to every friction coefficient in a wide range is optimal for none of them.

The counter-position was to measure instead of guess, and legged robotics is where it was made most sharply. Hwangbo et al., [Learning Agile and Dynamic Motor Skills for Legged Robots](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-agile-and-dynamic-motor-skills-for-legged-robots) (Science Robotics 2019; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.aau5872)) replaced the analytic actuator model of ANYmal's series-elastic drives with a neural network fitted to real joint data, mapping recent joint state history to realised torque. This reframed the identification target: not a handful of scalar parameters, but the input-output behaviour of a subsystem no one could write down. Tan et al., "Sim-to-Real: Learning Agile Locomotion for Quadruped Robots" (2018; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10332)) (historical reference, not in the list) made the complementary point that latency has to be simulated explicitly. Chebotar et al., "Closing the Sim-to-Real Loop" (2018; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05687)) (historical reference, not in the list) closed the loop the other way, updating the randomisation distribution itself from real rollouts rather than fixing it by hand.

The third move was to stop deciding offline and let the policy infer the environment online. Lee et al., "Learning Quadrupedal Locomotion over Challenging Terrain" (2020; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251)) (historical reference, not in the list) trained a privileged teacher with access to simulator ground truth and distilled it into a proprioception-only student, so that what could not be sensed was absorbed into a latent inferred from history. Kumar et al.'s RMA (2021; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04034)) (historical reference, not in the list) made that latent an explicit adaptation module estimated in a fraction of a second. This changed the training pipeline structurally — two stages, not one — and it changed the assumption: the policy no longer needs one behaviour valid everywhere, only a fast estimator of where it currently is.

Humanoid-specific work from 2024 onward is largely about what remains after all three. Evaluation moved first: [Humanoid-Gym](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-gym-reinforcement-learning-for-humanoid-robot-with-zero-shot-sim2real-t) made sim-to-sim — train in Isaac Gym, validate in MuJoCo — a routine gate before touching hardware, on the reasoning that a policy exploiting one engine's contact solver will fail another engine before it fails the robot. [PolySim](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/polysim-bridging-the-sim-to-real-gap-for-humanoid-control-via-multi-simulator-dy) turned that check into a training signal, running heterogeneous engines in parallel so simulator-specific bias is randomised over rather than merely detected, and reporting zero-shot transfer to a Unitree G1. The randomisation target was reconsidered too: [Joint Torque Space Perturbation Injection](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/sim-to-real-of-humanoid-locomotion-policies-via-joint-torque-space-perturbation-) argues that state-dependent perturbations at the torque input cover a wider class of reality gaps than randomising nominal physical parameters — an admission that parameter space was the wrong coordinate system for the uncertainty.

Identification came back sharpened rather than abandoned. [Sampling-Based System Identification with Active Exploration](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/sampling-based-system-identification-with-active-exploration-for-legged-robot-si) treats which real trajectories to collect as a design variable, optimising commands to maximise Fisher information of the resulting data — identification as an active, not passive, procedure. [DiffCoTune](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/diffcotune-differentiable-co-tuning-for-cross-domain-robot-control) uses differentiable simulation to tune simulator parameters and controller parameters jointly by alternating optimisation, dissolving the boundary between "fix the model" and "fix the controller". [Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Athletic Loco-Manipulation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/bridging-the-sim-to-real-gap-for-athletic-loco-manipulation) revives Hwangbo's actuator net for humanoids in an unsupervised form that does not require torque sensors. Most strikingly, [Towards bridging the gap: Systematic sim-to-real transfer for diverse legged robots](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/towards-bridging-the-gap-systematic-sim-to-real-transfer-for-diverse-legged-robo) reports transfer across ten-plus platforms using a physics-grounded PMSM motor model *without* randomising dynamics parameters — the strongest recent claim that good models can substitute for broad randomisation, though it is one result and should be read as such.

The remaining residual — what neither randomisation nor identification captures — is now attacked with real data directly. [ASAP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/asap-aligning-simulation-and-real-world-physics-for-learning-agile-humanoid-whol) (filed under loco-manipulation) learns a *delta action model* from real G1 rollouts, injects it into the simulator, fine-tunes the policy against the corrected dynamics, and deploys without it, reporting lower tracking error than system identification, domain randomisation and delta-dynamics baselines. [MOSAIC](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/mosaic-bridging-the-sim-to-real-gap-in-generalist-humanoid-motion-tracking-and-t) applies the residual idea at the interface level, training an additive residual module from a small amount of data and distilling it back into a general tracker, and reports this beating plain fine-tuning under realistic latency and noise. Fine-tuning on the robot has itself become tractable: [Robot Trains Robot](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robot-trains-robot-automatic-real-world-policy-adaptation-and-learning-for-human) uses a robot arm as an instrumented gantry supplying protection, resets, perturbations and reward so that a humanoid can be trained physically with minimal human intervention, and [Towards Bridging the Gap between Large-Scale Pretraining and Efficient Finetuning](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/towards-bridging-the-gap-between-large-scale-pretraining-and-efficient-finetunin) confines stochastic exploration to a physics-informed world model so the real robot only ever executes a deterministic policy — a direct answer to the objection that on-robot RL is unsafe.

My reading of the arc: the *object of correction* migrated from the observation distribution, to the parameter vector, to the actuator subsystem, to a latent inferred online, and finally to a learned residual on the action or dynamics — each step moving closer to the residual error itself and further from a physical interpretation of it. Whether that trade is worth it is not settled.

## Major technical transitions

Assumptions moved from "reality is inside the randomised support" to "reality is identifiable in the coordinates that matter" to "reality is whatever the residual model says it is". Identification targets moved from mass, inertia and friction scalars to actuator input-output maps, to motor-level electromechanical models, to action-space deltas. Pipelines moved from one-stage training, to teacher-student distillation with an online adaptation module, to train-deploy-correct-retrain loops needing real rollouts. Evaluation moved furthest: from "the demo worked" to sim-to-sim gates, cross-simulator agreement, and explicit online monitoring — [RAPT](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/rapt-model-predictive-out-of-distribution-detection-and-failure-diagnosis-for-si) learns a manifold of nominal execution, flags deviation at control rate, and reports root-cause classification over real failures from proprioception alone. Treating "is this policy still in distribution?" as a first-class question is new.

## Comparison of dominant approaches

| Family | Real-data requirement | Robustness gained | Conservativeness cost | Engineering effort | Typical failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Domain randomisation ([dynamics randomisation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06537), [PolySim](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/polysim-bridging-the-sim-to-real-gap-for-humanoid-control-via-multi-simulator-dy)) | None | Broad, distribution-wide | High — performance traded for coverage | Low code, high range-tuning | Reality outside the support; sluggish, over-damped behaviour |
| System identification / actuator models ([actuator nets](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-agile-and-dynamic-motor-skills-for-legged-robots), [SPI-Active](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/sampling-based-system-identification-with-active-exploration-for-legged-robot-si)) | Small, targeted excitation data | Sharp near the identified operating point | Low | High — rigs, excitation design, per-robot repeat | Identified regime not the deployment regime; drift as hardware wears |
| Teacher-student + online adaptation ([privileged learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251), [RMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04034), [contrastive adaptation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/contrastive-representation-learning-for-robust-sim-to-real-transfer-of-adaptive-)) | None | Adapts within the trained variation range | Moderate | Two-stage pipeline, distillation tuning | Latent estimator itself out of distribution; silent degradation |
| Real-world residual correction ([ASAP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/asap-aligning-simulation-and-real-world-physics-for-learning-agile-humanoid-whol), [MOSAIC](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/mosaic-bridging-the-sim-to-real-gap-in-generalist-humanoid-motion-tracking-and-t)) | Moderate — rollouts from a deployable policy | High for the specific motions covered | Low | Deploy-collect-retrain loop | Residual valid only where data was collected; needs a safe seed policy |
| Real-world fine-tuning ([Robot Trains Robot](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robot-trains-robot-automatic-real-world-policy-adaptation-and-learning-for-human), [pretrain-finetune](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/towards-bridging-the-gap-between-large-scale-pretraining-and-efficient-finetunin)) | Large, on-hardware | Highest on the deployed robot | Lowest | Highest — safety rig, resets, real reward | Hardware wear and safety; poor sample efficiency; overfit to one unit |

## Representative papers

- [Learning Agile and Dynamic Motor Skills for Legged Robots](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-agile-and-dynamic-motor-skills-for-legged-robots) — the actuator network: fit the drive's behaviour from real data instead of modelling it.
- [Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Athletic Loco-Manipulation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/bridging-the-sim-to-real-gap-for-athletic-loco-manipulation) — unsupervised actuator net for humanoids, no torque sensing required.
- [ASAP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/asap-aligning-simulation-and-real-world-physics-for-learning-agile-humanoid-whol) — delta-action model from real G1 rollouts, used to fine-tune inside a corrected simulator.
- [PolySim](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/polysim-bridging-the-sim-to-real-gap-for-humanoid-control-via-multi-simulator-dy) — randomise over simulators, not just parameters, to cancel engine-specific bias.
- [Sampling-Based System Identification with Active Exploration](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/sampling-based-system-identification-with-active-exploration-for-legged-robot-si) — picks excitation commands to maximise information in the real data collected.
- [DiffCoTune](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/diffcotune-differentiable-co-tuning-for-cross-domain-robot-control) — differentiable simulation tuning simulator and controller parameters together.
- [Joint Torque Space Perturbation Injection](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/sim-to-real-of-humanoid-locomotion-policies-via-joint-torque-space-perturbation-) — torque-space perturbation covers gaps parameter randomisation misses.
- [Robot Trains Robot](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robot-trains-robot-automatic-real-world-policy-adaptation-and-learning-for-human) — an arm-based rig automating protection, resets and reward for on-hardware training.
- [RAPT](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/rapt-model-predictive-out-of-distribution-detection-and-failure-diagnosis-for-si) — treats transfer failure as a monitoring and diagnosis problem, not only a training one.
- [ZEST](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/zest-zero-shot-embodied-skill-transfer-for-athletic-robot-control) — zero-shot motion-imitation deployment across Atlas, G1 and Spot from mocap, video and animation.

## Current research frontier

Four threads are active. Cheap proxies for reality: multi-simulator training in [PolySim](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/polysim-bridging-the-sim-to-real-gap-for-humanoid-control-via-multi-simulator-dy), sim-to-sim gates standard in tooling such as [MuJoCo Playground](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/mujoco-playground). Real-data residuals that are small, safe and reusable — [MOSAIC](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/mosaic-bridging-the-sim-to-real-gap-in-generalist-humanoid-motion-tracking-and-t) distils the residual back into a generalist rather than keeping a per-setup patch. Safe on-robot learning, where the interesting move is [confining exploration to a world model](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/towards-bridging-the-gap-between-large-scale-pretraining-and-efficient-finetunin) so hardware only executes deterministic actions. And the visual gap, handled largely outside this area — see [VIRAL](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/viral-visual-sim-to-real-at-scale-for-humanoid-loco-manipulation) and [pixel-to-action transfer](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/opening-the-sim-to-real-door-for-humanoid-pixel-to-action-policy-transfer) under loco-manipulation, and [vision-based dexterous manipulation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/sim-to-real-reinforcement-learning-for-vision-based-dexterous-manipulation-on-hu) under manipulation.

## Open problems and unresolved limitations

There is no accepted metric for the reality gap. Papers report task success after transfer, which conflates the size of the gap with the robustness of the policy and makes cross-paper comparison nearly impossible; a claim that method A transfers better than B is usually a claim about one robot, one task and one lab's calibration. Results are also reported per robot unit, and how far a policy tuned on one G1 transfers to another G1 — let alone across manufacturers — is rarely measured. Residual and delta-action methods trade interpretability for accuracy: a correction valid only where rollouts were collected is not a model, and nothing yet says when it stops being valid — the hole monitoring work like [RAPT](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/rapt-model-predictive-out-of-distribution-detection-and-failure-diagnosis-for-si) is beginning to fill. Whether motor-level modelling can genuinely replace randomisation, as [the multi-platform study](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/towards-bridging-the-gap-systematic-sim-to-real-transfer-for-diverse-legged-robo) suggests, is one line of evidence, not a settled finding. Finally, hardware is not stationary — backlash, wear and thermal state drift over a robot's life — yet almost all of this literature identifies or randomises once and assumes the model holds.
