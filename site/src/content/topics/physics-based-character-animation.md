## Definition and scope

Physics-based character animation synthesises motion by controlling a simulated articulated body through a physics engine rather than by playing back or blending recorded kinematics. The controller outputs joint torques or PD targets; contact, momentum and balance follow from simulation, not from keyframes. The research question is how to get motion that is simultaneously physically consistent, stylistically natural, and directable by a user or a task.

This list holds 35 entries spanning 2010 to early 2026. It sits in a humanoid-robot-learning collection because the fields share a substrate: a high-DoF floating-base humanoid, underactuated contact dynamics, and mocap as the dominant supervision signal. The differences matter as much — simulated characters have no torque ceiling, no actuator lag, no encoder noise, and perfect state observability.

## Why this problem matters

Much of the modern humanoid RL stack was prototyped here first: tracking rewards on retargeted mocap, adversarial style discriminators, reusable latent skill spaces, masked-inpainting command interfaces, and scene- and object-conditioned whole-body control all appear in graphics venues one to four years before their robotics counterparts. Because the setting removes hardware risk, reset cost and the sim-to-real gap, it is a fast-iteration testbed for representation and interface questions, leaving robotics the embodiment questions. Reading this area reads the robotics agenda one cycle early — and shows which ideas failed to cross over.

## Historical development

The first generation was hand-authored. [Hodgins et al., SIGGRAPH 1995](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/218380.218414) produced running, cycling and vaulting with finite-state machines and hand-tuned PD gains: compelling but non-transferable, since each new skill meant a new controller. [Faloutsos, van de Panne and Terzopoulos, SIGGRAPH 2001](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/383259.383287) attacked this by learning, per controller, a pre-condition classifier over the states from which it could succeed, so controllers could be sequenced automatically — the earliest statement of the composition problem [Learning to Ball](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-to-ball-composing-policies-for-long-horizon-basketball-moves) still addresses in 2025 with a learned soft router over subtask policies. [SIMBICON, SIGGRAPH 2007](https://doi.org/10.1145/1276377.1276509) then showed that a tiny balance feedback law, correcting the swing-hip target from centre-of-mass state, produced robust editable gait. (All three are historical references, not in the list.)

The optimisation branch matured in parallel. [SAMCON](https://doi.org/10.1145/1778765.1778865) (Liu, Yin, van de Panne, Shao and Xu, SIGGRAPH 2010; historical reference, not in the list) reconstructed contact-rich mocap by sampling-based search over open-loop control fragments, fixing the template — take a reference, search for a control signal reproducing it under physics — that motion imitation inherited wholesale. The oldest list entry, [Spatial relationship preserving character motion adaptation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/spatial-relationship-preserving-character-motion-adaptation), introduced the interaction mesh, a volumetric encoding of body-object spatial relations that retargeting should preserve; robotics rediscovered it fifteen years later, and interaction-preserving retargeting is now a live sub-literature in [loco-manipulation and whole-body control](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/topics/loco-manipulation-and-whole-body-control).

Deep RL arrived in two steps. [DeepLoco, SIGGRAPH 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3072959.3073602) (historical reference, not in the list) split control into a footstep tracker and a footstep planner; [DeepMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/deepmimic-example-guided-deep-reinforcement-learning-of-physics-based-character-) made the decisive move with a phase-indexed tracking reward on a single clip plus reference state initialisation and early termination. What mattered was that the same template yielded backflips, cartwheels and spin kicks with no per-skill engineering. Two contemporaneous entries mark paths largely not taken: [Learning Symmetric and Low-energy Locomotion](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-symmetric-and-low-energy-locomotion) got natural gait from hand-designed symmetry and energy objectives rather than data, and [Hierarchical visuomotor control of humanoids](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/hierarchical-visuomotor-control-of-humanoids) pre-trained low-level skills switched by a vision-conditioned high level, revisited only once latent spaces made the low level reusable, with multi-task machinery such as [PopArt](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/multi-task-deep-reinforcement-learning-with-popart) alongside.

DeepMimic imitated *a clip*, so a repertoire meant many policies and hand-designed transitions. [AMP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/amp-adversarial-motion-priors-for-stylized-physics-based-character-control) replaced the per-frame term with a discriminator separating policy from dataset transitions, turning imitation into distribution matching, so style became a soft prior composable with task reward. Robotics uptake was fast: within a year it substituted for hand-tuned locomotion rewards on a real quadruped ([Escontrela et al., IROS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15103)), after DeepMimic-style imitation plus domain adaptation had already transferred to hardware ([Peng et al., RSS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00784)) — both historical references, not in the list. [ASE](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/ase-large-scale-reusable-adversarial-skill-embeddings-for-physically-simulated-c) generalised AMP into a latent skill space pre-trained without task reward — later renamed "behaviour foundation model", visible here as [Zero-Shot Whole-Body Humanoid Control](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/zero-shot-whole-body-humanoid-control-via-behavioral-foundation-models); [CALM, SIGGRAPH 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02195) (historical reference, not in the list) added directability.

Consolidation followed. [Composite Motion Learning with Task Control](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/composite-motion-learning-with-task-control) used several body-part discriminators at once so composite motions emerge without authored composite references — an ancestor of the framing [AMOR](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/amor-adaptive-character-control-through-multi-objective-reinforcement-learning) pushed to weight-conditioned Pareto-front policies, motivated by sim-to-real retuning cost, while [ADD](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/add-physics-based-motion-imitation-with-adversarial-differential-discriminators) removed hand-tuned tracking rewards outright. [AdaptNet](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/adaptnet-policy-adaptation-for-physics-based-character-control) edited a trained policy's latent space for new styles, morphologies or environments, and [Perpetual Humanoid Control](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/perpetual-humanoid-control-for-real-time-simulated-avatars) made imitation infrastructure: one policy tracking large corpora in real time with fall recovery.

The generative turn came next. Motion diffusion gave diverse text-conditioned kinematics but violated physics ([PhysDiff, ICCV 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.02500), historical reference, not in the list). [CLoSD](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/closd-closing-the-loop-between-simulation-and-diffusion-for-multi-task-character) runs a diffusion planner inside the control loop, simulation state fed back at each replan; [MaskedMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/maskedmimic-unified-physics-based-character-control-through-masked-motion-inpain) recast control as masked inpainting over partial constraints — some joints, some keyframes, a text prompt — giving one policy a unified command interface; [PARC](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/parc-physics-based-augmentation-with-reinforcement-learning-for-character-contro) closed a data loop instead, a generator proposing motions on new terrain and a tracker correcting artefacts before they rejoin the dataset. [Iterative Closed-Loop Motion Synthesis](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/iterative-closed-loop-motion-synthesis-for-scaling-the-capabilities-of-humanoid-) extends that loop into 2026, reporting a 45% reduction in average failure rate on roughly a tenth of AMASS-scale data.

Interaction is the newest frontier. Early work scripted contact — [UniHSI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/unified-human-scene-interaction-via-prompted-chain-of-contacts) as language-prompted contact chains, [Box Loco-Manipulation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/hierarchical-planning-and-control-for-box-loco-manipulation) as one class with a planner-plus-controller stack. Objects then became first-class: [SkillMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/skillmimic-learning-basketball-interaction-skills-from-demonstrations) learned basketball skills from human-object demonstrations without skill-specific rewards; [InterMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/intermimic-towards-universal-whole-body-control-for-physics-based-human-object-i) targeted universal human-object control; [TokenHSI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/tokenhsi-unified-synthesis-of-physical-human-scene-interactions-through-task-tok) tokenised proprioception and tasks into one transformer policy; [MaskedManipulator](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/maskedmanipulator-versatile-whole-body-control-for-loco-manipulation) distilled a tracker into a generative policy taking object- or body-pose goals; [SimGenHOI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/simgenhoi-physically-realistic-whole-body-humanoid-object-interaction-via-genera) and [InterPrior](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/interprior-scaling-generative-control-for-physics-based-human-object-interaction) paired diffusion-transformer generation with RL correction; [Emergent Active Perception and Dexterity](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/emergent-active-perception-and-dexterity-of-simulated-humanoids-from-visual-rein) replaced privileged state with egocentric vision.

What transfers cleanly are representation choices independent of embodiment: tracking rewards, adversarial style priors, latent skill spaces, masked command interfaces, the generate-simulate-filter loop. What transfers poorly is everything the character gets for free. Torques are unbounded or generously bounded, so acrobatic results such as [Humanoid Robot Acrobatics](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-robot-acrobatics-utilizing-complete-articulated-rigid-body-dynamics) are simulation-only; contact is rigid, penalty-based and frictionally idealised; series elasticity, backlash, thermal derating and latency are unmodelled; and the character sees exact root pose and velocity, which no robot does. Bridging papers add one back — [RobotDancing](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robotdancing-residual-action-rl-enables-robust-long-horizon-humanoid-motion-trac) with residual actions over a reference, [RL from Physical Feedback](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/rl-from-physical-feedback-aligning-large-motion-models-with-humanoid-control) using a simulated tracker as a feasibility critic for a text-to-motion model, with reported real-robot deployment. That graphics supplies interfaces while robotics supplies embodiment constraints is my interpretation.

A parallel 2025-2026 thread runs backwards, from video to simulation (see the frontier below); if it succeeds, supervision moves from mocap studios to internet video — the same bet robotics is making.

## Major technical transitions

- Hand-authored FSM controllers give way to reference-driven optimisation, then RL on mocap.
- Imitation objective moves from per-frame tracking ([DeepMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/deepmimic-example-guided-deep-reinforcement-learning-of-physics-based-character-)) to distribution matching ([AMP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/amp-adversarial-motion-priors-for-stylized-physics-based-character-control)) and back toward learned per-motion discriminators ([ADD](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/add-physics-based-motion-imitation-with-adversarial-differential-discriminators)).
- Policies become reusable assets: latent skill spaces ([ASE](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/ase-large-scale-reusable-adversarial-skill-embeddings-for-physically-simulated-c)), adaptation ([AdaptNet](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/adaptnet-policy-adaptation-for-physics-based-character-control)), distillation into generative controllers ([MaskedManipulator](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/maskedmanipulator-versatile-whole-body-control-for-loco-manipulation)).
- Scope widens from the body alone to body-plus-object and body-plus-scene.
- Command interface generalises: clip index, task reward, latent, then masked constraints and text.
- Reward engineering recedes: multi-objective balancing ([Composite Motion Learning](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/composite-motion-learning-with-task-control)), weight-conditioned Pareto policies ([AMOR](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/amor-adaptive-character-control-through-multi-objective-reinforcement-learning)), discriminator-derived objectives ([ADD](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/add-physics-based-motion-imitation-with-adversarial-differential-discriminators)).
- Data stops being fixed: physics-corrected augmentation loops ([PARC](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/parc-physics-based-augmentation-with-reinforcement-learning-for-character-contro), [Iterative Closed-Loop Motion Synthesis](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/iterative-closed-loop-motion-synthesis-for-scaling-the-capabilities-of-humanoid-)).
- Observation moves from privileged state to egocentric vision ([Emergent Active Perception](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/emergent-active-perception-and-dexterity-of-simulated-humanoids-from-visual-rein)).

## Comparison of dominant approaches

| Family | Data requirement | Motion quality | Controllability | Generalisation | Transfer to hardware |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trajectory optimisation / hand-designed controllers | None or one reference | Exact, often stiff | Very high; interpretable | Poor; retune per skill | Good in principle, needs accurate models |
| Motion-imitation RL ([DeepMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/deepmimic-example-guided-deep-reinforcement-learning-of-physics-based-character-), [PHC](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/perpetual-humanoid-control-for-real-time-simulated-avatars)) | One clip to a mocap corpus | High fidelity to reference | Via reference choice; weak for novel goals | Interpolates in-corpus | Works on hardware if the reference is feasible |
| Adversarial motion priors ([AMP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/amp-adversarial-motion-priors-for-stylized-physics-based-character-control), [ADD](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/add-physics-based-motion-imitation-with-adversarial-differential-discriminators)) | Unsegmented motion sets | Natural style, coarser tracking | Task reward composes with style | Style-level, not pose-level | Strong; used on real legged robots ([Escontrela et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15103)) |
| Latent skill spaces ([ASE](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/ase-large-scale-reusable-adversarial-skill-embeddings-for-physically-simulated-c), [BFM](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/zero-shot-whole-body-humanoid-control-via-behavioral-foundation-models)) | Large observation-only datasets | Inherited from the prior | High-level; fine detail hard to specify | Strong downstream, sometimes zero-shot | Mostly simulation-validated |

## Representative papers

- [DeepMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/deepmimic-example-guided-deep-reinforcement-learning-of-physics-based-character-) — the tracking-reward template, with reference state initialisation and early termination, the area builds on.
- [AMP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/amp-adversarial-motion-priors-for-stylized-physics-based-character-control) — replaces per-frame tracking with a transition discriminator, making style a composable prior.
- [ASE](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/ase-large-scale-reusable-adversarial-skill-embeddings-for-physically-simulated-c) — pre-trains a reusable latent skill space; tasks are then solved over latents.
- [Perpetual Humanoid Control](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/perpetual-humanoid-control-for-real-time-simulated-avatars) — real-time corpus-scale tracking with fall recovery, reused by later systems.
- [MaskedMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/maskedmimic-unified-physics-based-character-control-through-masked-motion-inpain) — recasts control as masked inpainting, unifying many command modalities.
- [CLoSD](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/closd-closing-the-loop-between-simulation-and-diffusion-for-multi-task-character) — puts a text-conditioned diffusion planner inside the control loop.
- [TokenHSI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/tokenhsi-unified-synthesis-of-physical-human-scene-interactions-through-task-tok) — task tokenisation with a shared proprioception token gives extensible scene interaction.
- [AMOR](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/amor-adaptive-character-control-through-multi-objective-reinforcement-learning) — weight-conditioned Pareto-front policy, motivated by sim-to-real retuning cost.
- [Feature-Based vs GAN-Based Learning from Demonstrations](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/feature-based-vs-gan-based-learning-from-demonstrations-when-and-why) — survey arguing the two paradigms are complementary and converging.

## Current research frontier

Four directions are active. First, scaling interaction priors: [InterPrior](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/interprior-scaling-generative-control-for-physics-based-human-object-interaction) combines large-scale imitation pretraining, perturbation augmentation and RL finetuning into a latent manifold that generalises to unseen objects. Second, closed-loop data generation, with a physics tracker as automatic filter and corrector ([PARC](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/parc-physics-based-augmentation-with-reinforcement-learning-for-character-contro), [SimGenHOI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/simgenhoi-physically-realistic-whole-body-humanoid-object-interaction-via-genera), [Iterative Closed-Loop Motion Synthesis](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/iterative-closed-loop-motion-synthesis-for-scaling-the-capabilities-of-humanoid-)). Third, high-dynamic coverage: [FARM](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/farm-frame-accelerated-augmentation-and-residual-mixture-of-experts-for-physics-) documents that controllers strong on everyday motion still fail on explosive actions, and reports a 42.8% reduction in tracking failure on its own benchmark using frame-accelerated augmentation and a residual mixture-of-experts. Fourth, real-to-sim from ordinary video ([CRISP](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/crisp-contact-guided-real2sim-from-monocular-video-with-planar-scene-primitives), [PhysHMR](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/physhmr-learning-humanoid-control-policies-from-vision-for-physically-plausible-), [Mimic2DM](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-to-control-physically-simulated-3d-characters-via-generating-and-mimick)), which would remove the mocap-studio dependency shared with robotics.

## Open problems and unresolved limitations

Evaluation is the weakest link. Naturalness is judged by user studies and by tracking error against references the method may itself have generated, and several 2025 entries introduce their own benchmarks ([FARM](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/farm-frame-accelerated-augmentation-and-residual-mixture-of-experts-for-physics-)), making cross-paper comparison difficult. Contact modelling is a second gap: penalty-based rigid contact with fixed friction underpins essentially all results here, so grasping, sliding and deformable interaction are approximated rather than simulated. Third, interface generality has not been shown under partial observability — nearly all controllers assume exact root state, and only a few ([Emergent Active Perception](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/emergent-active-perception-and-dexterity-of-simulated-humanoids-from-visual-rein), [PhysHMR](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/physhmr-learning-humanoid-control-policies-from-vision-for-physically-plausible-)) remove it. Fourth, torque and bandwidth limits are usually absent, so high-dynamic results are not evidence about hardware feasibility; residual and feasibility-filtering methods ([RobotDancing](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robotdancing-residual-action-rl-enables-robust-long-horizon-humanoid-motion-trac), [RL from Physical Feedback](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/rl-from-physical-feedback-aligning-large-motion-models-with-humanoid-control)) exist because that inference does not hold. Finally, unmodelled compliance — series elasticity, soft-tissue deformation, backlash — shapes how real human motion looks, and adding it trades against the simulation throughput the RL pipeline needs.
