## Definition and scope

This area covers the measurement, representation and generation of human movement as *kinematics*: capturing motion with optical markers, inertial sensors or cameras; fitting a parametric body to those observations; and synthesising new motion from examples, text, keyframes or trajectories. The object of study is the motion signal, not the controller that executes it. Physics enters as a plausibility constraint, not the primary formalism — that belongs to [physics-based character animation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/topics/physics-based-character-animation), and the downstream robot problem to [loco-manipulation and whole-body control](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/topics/loco-manipulation-and-whole-body-control).

The 29 entries here skew toward the last four years, toward generative synthesis and sparse-sensor capture; earlier graphics and vision work is cited externally and marked as such.

## Why this problem matters

Humanoid robots have roughly human morphology, so the cheapest large source of behaviour data for them is recordings of humans. Most modern humanoid whole-body controllers are trained, directly or indirectly, on retargeted human motion capture: the reward is tracking error against a reference trajectory, and the reference comes from this field. That makes motion analysis and synthesis the *supply chain* of humanoid learning rather than a neighbouring topic.

There are two distinct products. The first is data: corpora, body models, and estimators that turn video or a handful of body-worn sensors into joint trajectories. The second is priors: generative models that produce new motion on demand from text, waypoints or partial keyframes, so a controller can be trained on far more behaviour than was ever captured. Both share a defect. A kinematic trajectory is not a feasible robot trajectory — it came from a body with different link lengths, mass distribution, joint limits and effectively unlimited actuation. Closing that gap is the recurring theme below.

## Historical development

The field begins with a splicing problem. Optical marker capture produced clean joint trajectories but only of what was actually performed, so the first synthesis question was how to recombine clips. Kovar, Gleicher and Pighin's motion graphs (SIGGRAPH 2002; [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/566570.566605)) (historical reference, not in the list) answered it structurally: build a directed graph whose edges are clips and whose nodes are frames similar enough to transition through, then reduce synthesis to graph search. The second answer, motion matching, dropped the precomputed graph and searched the whole database every frame for the pose best matching the current state and desired future trajectory — simple and responsive, but requiring the entire corpus resident in memory. [Learned motion matching](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learned-motion-matching) replaced the database and the search with small neural networks reproducing the same behaviour, cutting memory by roughly two orders of magnitude while preserving the algorithm's semantics. The lineage matters for robotics because it is where the idea of a *motion prior as a queryable object* was established.

A second thread made motion comparable across subjects and datasets. Retargeting was posed early as constrained optimisation by Gleicher (SIGGRAPH 1998; [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/280814.280820)) (historical reference, not in the list): preserve semantic constraints such as foot contact while changing skeleton proportions. The decisive step was a shared parametric body. SMPL ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2816795.2818013)) gave a differentiable learned skinned model separating shape and pose blend shapes, and [AMASS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03278) (both historical references, not in the list) fit SMPL to dozens of heterogeneous marker datasets, producing one unified corpus. AMASS is the most consequential artefact here for humanoids: it is the training set behind simulated motion imitators such as Perpetual Humanoid Control ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06456)) (historical reference, not in the list), which in turn seed real-robot tracking policies.

Capture then escaped the lab along two routes. From video, HMR ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06584)) regressed SMPL parameters end-to-end with an adversarial pose prior, and later transformer systems such as Humans in 4D ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20091)) (both historical references, not in the list) pushed in-the-wild reconstruction and tracking. From sparse wearables, Deep Inertial Poser ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04703)) (historical reference, not in the list) showed six IMUs suffice for full-body pose, and consumer VR made the constraint harsher still — head and two hands. [AvatarPoser](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/avatarposer-articulated-full-body-pose-tracking-from-sparse-motion-sensing) treated that as a transformer sequence problem with an inverse-kinematics refinement stage; [MANIKIN](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/manikin-biomechanically-accurate-neural-inverse-kinematics-for-human-motion-esti) added biomechanical constraints to the neural IK; and [EgoPoser](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egoposer-robust-real-time-egocentric-pose-estimation-from-sparse-and-intermitten) attacked the failure that appears the moment you leave a demo, where hands leave the headset's field of view and global position must be recovered without a fixed capture volume. The same world-grounding question drives [FRAME](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/frame-floor-aligned-representation-for-avatar-motion-from-egocentric-video). This is exactly the regime a humanoid teleoperator sits in, which is why sparse-sensor tracking is not a peripheral concern.

Generation, meanwhile, moved from search to learning. Convolutional autoencoders over motion manifolds (SIGGRAPH 2016; [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2897824.2925975)), phase-functioned networks for terrain-adaptive locomotion (SIGGRAPH 2017; [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3072959.3073663)), motion VAEs for autoregressive character control (SIGGRAPH 2020; [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3386569.3392422)) and robust motion in-betweening ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04942)) (all historical references, not in the list) established that a learned latent space could stand in for a clip database. Language conditioning arrived with paired data: [Generating Diverse and Natural 3D Human Motions From Text](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/generating-diverse-and-natural-3d-human-motions-from-text) introduced HumanML3D (14,616 clips, 44,970 descriptions) with a temporal VAE and a text-to-length sampler, and that dataset became the benchmark the next generation was measured on.

Diffusion then took over, and the interesting part is what it changed about *control* rather than about sample quality. MDM ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14916)) (historical reference, not in the list) established the recipe of predicting the clean motion rather than the noise, with geometric losses. [Guided Motion Diffusion](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/guided-motion-diffusion-for-controllable-human-motion-synthesis) and [OmniControl](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/omnicontrol-control-any-joint-at-any-time-for-human-motion-generation) exploited the fact that sampling is iterative and so admits injected spatial constraints — trajectories, obstacles, any joint at any time — precisely the interface a robot planner wants, and [Flexible Motion In-betweening](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/flexible-motion-in-betweening-with-diffusion-models) folded keyframe completion into it. Two structural limits followed: diffusion is offline and fixed-length, and it is slow. [TEDi](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/tedi-temporally-entangled-diffusion-for-long-term-motion-synthesis) assigned different noise levels to different frames of a rolling buffer, converting the sampler into a stream; [Taming Diffusion Probabilistic Models for Character Control](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/taming-diffusion-probabilistic-models-for-character-control) targeted real-time interactive control. Meanwhile [Example-based Motion Synthesis via Generative Motion Matching](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/example-based-motion-synthesis-via-generative-motion-matching) showed a training-free patch-based scheme still competes when only a handful of examples exist: the search paradigm did not die, it specialised.

The physical-plausibility gap was then attacked from inside the generative model rather than downstream. [PhysDiff](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/physdiff-physics-guided-human-motion-diffusion-model) inserted a physics-based projection step into the denoising loop, so intermediate samples are corrected by a simulated character instead of post-processed. [PRIMAL](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/primal-physically-reactive-and-interactive-motor-model-for-avatar-learning) pretrains on short clips and then generates unbounded motion reacting to physical interaction at run time. My reading is that this is where the field stops being purely kinematic: the same prior a text-to-motion user wants is being asked to satisfy contact and balance constraints, which is what a humanoid controller needs. It also explains why humanoid papers such as ExBody ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16796)) (historical reference, not in the list) spend so much effort filtering retargeted mocap — the upstream prior knows nothing about torque limits, so the downstream policy must reject what it cannot execute.

The most recent phase is scale plus unification. [Scaling Large Motion Models](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/scaling-large-motion-models-with-million-level-human-motions) built MotionLib, reported as at least 15x larger than prior counterparts, with a lossless tokeniser; [Go to Zero](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/go-to-zero-towards-zero-shot-motion-generation-with-million-scale-data) reports over 2,000 hours and 2 million text-paired sequences with a 7B-parameter model aimed at zero-shot generation; [Being-M0.5](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/being-m0-5-a-real-time-controllable-vision-language-motion-model) reports a 5M-sequence corpus with part-level annotation and real-time controllable generation. Separately, [GENMO](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/genmo-a-generalist-model-for-human-motion) argues estimation and generation are the same problem — estimation being generation constrained to match observations — and trains one model for both. [Kimodo](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/kimodo-scaling-controllable-human-motion-generation) trains a two-stage diffusion denoiser on 700 hours of production optical mocap, separating root from body prediction to suppress floating and foot skating, and explicitly targets humanoid simulation and robot training. That framing — a graphics-quality motion model advertised as a robot data source — is the clearest sign the two communities have converged.

## Major technical transitions

Representation moved from raw joint angles on ad-hoc skeletons, to a shared parametric body (SMPL) that makes datasets composable, to discrete motion tokens that make language-model machinery applicable. Sensing moved from marker-based studio capture, through monocular video regression, to sparse head-and-hand tracking and egocentric cameras in the wild. Synthesis moved from graph search over clips, to autoregressive latent-variable models, to iterative denoising whose intermediate states accept constraints, and back toward tokenised autoregressive models at language-model scale. Conditioning moved from trajectory and terrain, to text, to joint-level spatial constraints and part-level instructions. Plausibility moved from post-hoc cleanup, to physics in the sampling loop, to datasets recording contact and force directly.

## Comparison of dominant approaches

| Family | Data requirement | Controllability | Physical plausibility | Usefulness as robot training data | Typical failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Motion graphs / motion matching ([2002](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/566570.566605), [2020](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learned-motion-matching)) | A curated clip database covering the desired behaviours | High and immediate for trajectory following; nothing outside the database | Inherited from capture, so per-frame poses are real but transitions can be non-physical | High per-clip fidelity; coverage limited to what was recorded | Foot skating and popping at transitions; silent failure when no matching clip exists |
| Statistical body models + fitting ([SMPL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2816795.2818013), [AMASS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03278), [HMR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06584), [AvatarPoser](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/avatarposer-articulated-full-body-pose-tracking-from-sparse-motion-sensing)) | Markers, IMUs or video plus a fitted template | Not generative; controllability is the observation itself | None enforced; global translation and contact are the weak points | Very high, since this is how raw capture becomes a usable corpus | Depth and scale ambiguity, foot-ground penetration, drift in world coordinates |
| Deep generative kinematic models ([2016](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2897824.2925975), [motion VAEs](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3386569.3392422), [PRIMAL](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/primal-physically-reactive-and-interactive-motor-model-for-avatar-learning)) | Medium corpora; compresses the database into weights | Latent or autoregressive conditioning; smooth but indirect | Learned regularity only, no contact guarantees | Good for augmenting coverage; needs filtering | Over-smoothed mean poses, drift and divergence in long autoregressive rollouts |
| Text-conditioned diffusion / tokenised models ([MDM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14916), [OmniControl](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/omnicontrol-control-any-joint-at-any-time-for-human-motion-generation), [MotionMillion](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/go-to-zero-towards-zero-shot-motion-generation-with-million-scale-data)) | Large paired text-motion corpora, the binding constraint | Highest semantic controllability; spatial constraints injectable at sampling | Weak; contact and dynamics are not modelled | Attractive for scaling behaviour variety, but samples need physical vetting | Sliding contacts, ground penetration, semantically plausible but dynamically impossible motion |
| Physics-aware synthesis ([PhysDiff](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/physdiff-physics-guided-human-motion-diffusion-model), [PRIMAL](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/primal-physically-reactive-and-interactive-motor-model-for-avatar-learning)) | Generative corpus plus a simulator and a tracking policy | Constrained by what the simulated body can achieve | Highest of the five families, by construction | Best aligned with controller training, at the cost of simulator fidelity | Simulator and model mismatch; the projection policy fails on out-of-distribution motion |

## Representative papers

- [Learned motion matching](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learned-motion-matching) — replaces the motion database and nearest-neighbour search with small networks, keeping motion-matching behaviour without keeping the data.
- [Generating Diverse and Natural 3D Human Motions From Text](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/generating-diverse-and-natural-3d-human-motions-from-text) — introduces HumanML3D, the paired corpus most later text-to-motion work is benchmarked on.
- [AvatarPoser](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/avatarposer-articulated-full-body-pose-tracking-from-sparse-motion-sensing) — full-body pose from head and hand tracking only, the canonical sparse-sensor formulation.
- [EgoPoser](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egoposer-robust-real-time-egocentric-pose-estimation-from-sparse-and-intermitten) — intermittent hand visibility and unbounded global position: the realistic version of that problem.
- [PhysDiff](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/physdiff-physics-guided-human-motion-diffusion-model) — physics projection inside the diffusion loop rather than as post-processing.
- [OmniControl](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/omnicontrol-control-any-joint-at-any-time-for-human-motion-generation) — spatial control of any joint at any timestep, the interface closest to what a robot planner needs.
- [TEDi](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/tedi-temporally-entangled-diffusion-for-long-term-motion-synthesis) — per-frame noise levels in a rolling buffer turn diffusion into a streaming synthesiser.
- [Example-based Motion Synthesis via Generative Motion Matching](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/example-based-motion-synthesis-via-generative-motion-matching) — training-free synthesis from very few examples; search-based methods retain a niche.
- [Scaling Large Motion Models with Million-Level Human Motions](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/scaling-large-motion-models-with-million-level-human-motions) — MotionLib plus a compact tokeniser; an explicit scaling study for motion.
- [GENMO](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/genmo-a-generalist-model-for-human-motion) — treats estimation as constrained generation and trains a single model for both.
- [Kimodo](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/kimodo-scaling-controllable-human-motion-generation) — 700 hours of production mocap, root/body decomposition against foot skating, aimed at humanoid simulation.

## Current research frontier

Four directions are active. Scale: million-sequence corpora and multi-billion-parameter models pursuing zero-shot generalisation to unseen and compositional motions ([MotionMillion](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/go-to-zero-towards-zero-shot-motion-generation-with-million-scale-data), [Being-M0.5](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/being-m0-5-a-real-time-controllable-vision-language-motion-model)). Interaction: motion modelled jointly with objects and scenes rather than in isolation — [PICO](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/pico-reconstructing-3d-people-in-contact-with-objects), [efficient monocular human-object interaction reconstruction](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/efficient-and-scalable-monocular-human-object-interaction-motion-reconstruction), [WHOLE](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/whole-world-grounded-hand-object-lifted-from-egocentric-videos) for world-grounded hand-object motion from egocentric video, and [Diffusion Forcing for multi-agent interaction](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/diffusion-forcing-for-multi-agent-interaction-sequence-modeling). Capture escaping the studio: [EmbodMocap](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/embodmocap-in-the-wild-4d-human-scene-reconstruction-for-embodied-agents) collects metric-scale 4D human-and-scene data from two moving phones and names humanoid sim-to-real as a target application, while [ClimbingCap](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/climbingcap-multi-modal-dataset-and-method-for-rock-climbing-in-world-coordinate) and [climber force estimation from video](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/climber-force-and-motion-estimation-from-video) push into extreme non-locomotor contact and into estimating forces, not only poses. Precision control: [implicit Bézier motion models](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/implicit-bezier-motion-model-for-precise-spatial-and-temporal-control) and [control operators for interactive character animation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/control-operators-for-interactive-character-animation) address the complaint that large generative models are hard to steer at fine granularity.

## Open problems and unresolved limitations

The retargeting and feasibility gap remains the central unsolved issue for robotics. A generated motion satisfies a learned prior over human kinematics, not a torque, velocity or contact-force budget for a specific machine; the standard remedy is aggressive filtering plus a simulated tracking stage, which discards much of the generated diversity and hides the problem rather than solving it. Force is largely unmeasured — most corpora record positions only, so the quantity determining whether a motion is executable is absent from the training data, which makes work like [climber force estimation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/climber-force-and-motion-estimation-from-video) interesting beyond its domain. Evaluation is weak: FID-style scores on HumanML3D reward distributional similarity to a modest benchmark and say nothing about physical validity or transfer to a robot. Data composition is opaque at the new scale: million-sequence corpora are assembled from mixed sources with heterogeneous quality and annotation, so reported zero-shot gains are hard to attribute between data, tokenisation and model size. Controllability and real-time operation remain in tension, since the constraint-injection property that makes diffusion steerable is tied to an iterative sampler expensive to run in a control loop. Finally, hands, faces and fine manipulation are still poorly covered relative to gross body motion, limiting these priors for the loco-manipulation problems humanoids are actually being built for.
