## Definition and scope

This area covers the estimation of a legged robot's own state: the pose, velocity and orientation of its floating base, the sensor biases corrupting those quantities, contact and slip status at the feet, and — at the edge of the definition — internal quantities such as joint torque that are not directly measured. The object of study is the estimator, not the controller consuming it, and not the environment map except where mapping corrects drift. Proprioceptive fusion of IMU and leg kinematics sits at the centre; visual-inertial odometry and SLAM appear as modules supplying exteroceptive corrections rather than as a separate research programme.

This collection holds only 11 entries here — a sampling artefact, not a statement about the field. Legged state estimation is a mature subfield with two decades of dedicated literature, its own benchmarks and several long-running open-source stacks; the list captures a few load-bearing method papers, four library entries and three recent learning-flavoured contributions. Work below that falls outside those 11 entries is cited externally and marked as such.

## Why this problem matters

A legged robot has no sensor attached to the world. Its base is floating: joint encoders measure the limbs relative to the trunk and an IMU measures proper acceleration and angular rate, but nothing in that set observes absolute position or heading. Integrating IMU data gives position error growing cubically with time; the only thing that arrests it is the geometric constraint that a foot in contact is momentarily stationary. Every proprioceptive estimator for legged machines is, at bottom, a way of exploiting that constraint. The consequences are practical: whole-body controllers need base velocity at kilohertz rates and are unforgiving of lag, and perception stacks need a base pose good enough to register successive depth frames. When the estimator degrades the failure is a fall rather than a warning, which is why consistency — not just accuracy — has become a first-class evaluation criterion.

## Historical development

The starting point of the modern line is an observability argument. Because absolute position and yaw are unobservable from IMU and kinematics alone, a well-posed estimator must not claim to know them; it can only bound the drift of a local frame. Bloesch et al.'s "State Estimation for Legged Robots - Consistent Fusion of Leg Kinematics and IMU" (RSS 2012; [proceedings](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss08/p03.html)) (historical reference, not in the list) gave the template that most later work varies: propagate an IMU-driven process model, augment the state with the absolute positions of the feet currently in contact — spawned and marginalised as the gait proceeds — and treat forward kinematics as a measurement relating base pose to those foot states. Crucially, the construction leaves position and yaw unobservable by design, which is the honest formulation rather than a defect.

Humanoids forced two extensions. Flat feet make contact a six-degree-of-freedom constraint rather than a point, so foot orientation had to enter the state; Rotella et al.'s "State Estimation for a Humanoid Robot" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.5450)) worked this out with quaternion-based filtering. And because proprioception can only bound drift, exteroception was added to remove it: Fallon et al.'s drift-free humanoid estimation fusing kinematics, inertial and lidar sensing ([IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7041346/)) used a prior map to pin the local frame (both historical references, not in the list). Meanwhile the rigid-non-slipping-contact assumption underlying the whole construction was the weak link, and work such as Camurri et al.'s probabilistic contact estimation and impact detection ([IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7815333/)) (historical reference, not in the list) moved contact from a binary force threshold to an inferred quantity feeding the filter's measurement gating.

The second transition was mathematical rather than sensory. A quaternion-parameterised EKF linearises around the current estimate, so its error dynamics depend on that estimate; for the legged problem this produces the known inconsistency in which the filter acquires spurious confidence about unobservable directions. Barrau and Bonnabel's [The invariant extended Kalman filter as a stable observer](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/the-invariant-extended-kalman-filter-as-a-stable-observer) supplied the fix: if the process model is group-affine on a matrix Lie group, the error defined in the group's own geometry propagates by a log-linear equation independent of the estimated trajectory, buying local convergence guarantees and, in practice, better consistency. [Contact-Aided Invariant Extended Kalman Filtering for Robot State Estimation](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/contact-aided-invariant-extended-kalman-filtering-for-robot-state-estimation) carried that into legged robotics by placing base orientation, velocity, position and contact-foot positions in a single group and showing forward kinematics to be a right-invariant observation of it. The reported payoff — much weaker sensitivity to initialisation error — matters because a filter that recovers from a bad start is one you can restart mid-experiment.

The third transition traded recursion for optimisation. Filters commit to a linearisation once; smoothers keep a window of past states and relinearise. Factor graphs made that affordable: [GTSAM](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/gtsam-factor-graphs-for-sensor-fusion-in-robotics) packages the iSAM2 incremental solver of Kaess et al. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364911430419)), and Forster et al.'s on-manifold IMU preintegration ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02363)) (both historical references, not in the list) compressed high-rate inertial data into single relative-motion factors. [Legged Robot State-Estimation Through Combined Forward Kinematic and Preintegrated Contact Factors](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/legged-robot-state-estimation-through-combined-forward-kinematic-and-preintegrat) did the analogous compression for legs, turning a contact interval into one factor whose noise grows with contact duration — an explicit statement of how much foot slip is tolerated, replacing the binary rigid-contact assumption.

In parallel, visual-inertial estimation matured into infrastructure that legged stacks consume rather than rebuild. ORB-SLAM (Mur-Artal et al., [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TRO.2015.2463671)) and VINS-Mono (Qin et al., [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03852)) (both historical references, not in the list) established the two dominant designs — keyframe bundle adjustment with loop closure, and sliding-window tight visual-inertial fusion — and their successors appear in this list as libraries: [ORB-SLAM3](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/orb-slam3-an-accurate-open-source-library-for-visual-visual-inertial-and-multi-m), [VINS-Fusion](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/vins-fusion-an-optimization-based-multi-sensor-state-estimator) and [Kimera](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/kimera-an-open-source-library-for-real-time-metric-semantic-localization-and-map). Their presence as undated entries is itself informative: the research question shifted from building an estimator to selecting one. Evaluation followed. [An Empirical Evaluation of Four Off-the-Shelf Proprietary VIO Systems](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/an-empirical-evaluation-of-four-off-the-shelf-proprietary-visual-inertial-odomet) benchmarks ARKit, ARCore, RealSense T265 and ZED 2 indoors and outdoors on stability, consistency and accuracy — a paper about component selection, not algorithm design. On the proprioceptive side, comparisons such as the study of flat-foot estimators on iCub ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07765)) (historical reference, not in the list) began reporting filter consistency alongside trajectory accuracy, the metric the invariant-filtering argument predicts should separate the designs.

The most recent shift is learning, entering in three distinct places rather than as a wholesale replacement. It appears as a learned front end supplying a measurement the physics does not give cleanly — Buchanan et al.'s learned inertial odometry for dynamic legged robots ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00789)) (historical reference, not in the list) is the early example, and [AutoOdom](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/autoodom-learning-auto-regressive-proprioceptive-odometry-for-legged-locomotion) the current one, training an autoregressive proprioceptive odometry model on large-scale simulation plus limited real data and reporting substantial trajectory-error reductions over a learned baseline on a Booster T1 humanoid, targeting the GPS-denied, visually degraded regime where VIO fails. It appears as a hybrid around a model-based core: [InEKFormer](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/inekformer-a-hybrid-state-estimator-for-humanoid-robots) combines an InEKF with a transformer, evaluated against InEKF and KalmanNet baselines on RH5 humanoid data. And it appears as a way to estimate quantities previously assumed sensed: [PINN-UKF](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/physics-informed-neural-networks-with-unscented-kalman-filter-for-sensorless-joi) learns friction and feeds it into a UKF to run whole-body torque control on ergoCub without joint torque sensors. My reading is that these share a pattern: the learned component absorbs one unmodelled term — IMU-to-motion structure, residual filter error, friction — while the geometric skeleton is retained. That is a more conservative posture than learning took in locomotion control, reflecting the fact that an estimator has correctness properties a policy does not.

## Major technical transitions

Assumptions moved from rigid, non-slipping point contact to inferred contact probability and duration-dependent, slip-aware noise. Sensing moved from IMU plus encoders, to added force/torque, to tightly coupled camera and lidar, and recently back toward proprioception-only designs for perceptually degraded settings. Formulation moved from Euclidean-error EKFs, through quaternion and manifold parameterisations, to Lie-group invariant errors and then to sparse nonlinear least squares over a window. Computation moved from fixed-cost recursion to incremental relinearisation with variable cost, and now to neural inference with fixed cost but opaque failure behaviour. Evaluation moved from short mocap-room runs to trajectory error over long runs, then to consistency metrics, and — in the library era — to benchmarking whole off-the-shelf systems.

## Comparison of dominant approaches

| Family | Core assumptions | Drift behaviour | Compute | Robustness to slip | Typical failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quaternion/Euclidean EKF ([Bloesch 2012](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss08/p03.html), [Rotella 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.5450)) | Rigid non-slipping contact; small errors around the current estimate | Position and yaw drift without bound; local frame usable | Low, fixed | Poor without explicit outlier gating | Overconfident covariance in unobservable directions; divergence after a bad start |
| Invariant filtering ([Barrau & Bonnabel](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/the-invariant-extended-kalman-filter-as-a-stable-observer), [contact-aided InEKF](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/contact-aided-invariant-extended-kalman-filtering-for-robot-state-estimation)) | Group-affine dynamics; contact as right-invariant observation | Same unobservable directions, but consistent covariance | Low, comparable to EKF | Better conditioned, though slip still violates the measurement model | Model no longer group-affine once extra terms are added; still needs contact detection |
| Factor-graph smoothing ([preintegrated contact factors](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/legged-robot-state-estimation-through-combined-forward-kinematic-and-preintegrat), [GTSAM](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/gtsam-factor-graphs-for-sensor-fusion-in-robotics)) | Gaussian factors; slip absorbed into duration-dependent noise | Drift reducible retroactively; loop closure possible | Higher and variable; depends on window and relinearisation | Best of the model-based options, since slip is modelled not denied | Latency spikes; sensitivity to wrong noise scaling; non-Gaussian outliers |
| VIO/SLAM-coupled ([ORB-SLAM3](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/orb-slam3-an-accurate-open-source-library-for-visual-visual-inertial-and-multi-m), [VINS-Fusion](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/vins-fusion-an-optimization-based-multi-sensor-state-estimator), [Kimera](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/kimera-an-open-source-library-for-real-time-metric-semantic-localization-and-map)) | Static, textured, well-lit scene; reliable extrinsics | Bounded within a revisited map; resets on relocalisation | Highest; usually a separate thread or computer | Independent of contact, so slip is not the failure mode | Feature loss in smoke, darkness or motion blur; scale and map corruption |
| Learned / hybrid ([AutoOdom](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/autoodom-learning-auto-regressive-proprioceptive-odometry-for-legged-locomotion), [InEKFormer](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/inekformer-a-hybrid-state-estimator-for-humanoid-robots), [PINN-UKF](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/physics-informed-neural-networks-with-unscented-kalman-filter-for-sensorless-joi)) | Test-time motions resemble training distribution | Reported improvements over baselines; long-horizon behaviour less characterised | Fixed inference cost, often GPU or NPU | Learned implicitly from data rather than assumed away | Silent degradation off-distribution; no covariance one can trust |

## Current research frontier

Three directions are visible in the 2025 entries: proprioception-only estimation revisited with sequence models rather than filters, motivated by settings where cameras fail ([AutoOdom](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/autoodom-learning-auto-regressive-proprioceptive-odometry-for-legged-locomotion)); hybrids that keep a geometric filter and learn its residual ([InEKFormer](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/inekformer-a-hybrid-state-estimator-for-humanoid-robots)); and a widening of "state" to actuation-side quantities ([PINN-UKF](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/physics-informed-neural-networks-with-unscented-kalman-filter-for-sensorless-joi)). The interesting question for the second is not accuracy but whether the hybrid inherits the invariant filter's consistency guarantees, which the reported baseline comparison does not by itself settle.

## Open problems and unresolved limitations

Slip remains the structural weakness. Every proprioceptive estimator infers motion from a stationarity assumption that a slipping foot violates, and current practice handles this by inflating noise or gating measurements rather than by modelling the slip. Compliant ground compounds it, since the contact point moves even when the foot does not, and contact detection remains an upstream dependency whose errors propagate.

Uncertainty calibration is unresolved for the learned families. A filter reports a covariance with a defined meaning; a network reports a trajectory. Until learned estimators emit calibrated uncertainty, downstream controllers cannot fuse them the way they fuse filter output. Related is evaluation: trajectory error on curated runs does not reveal how an estimator behaves on the rare transient — an impact, a mid-air phase, a saturated IMU — that actually causes falls, and long-horizon and out-of-distribution benchmarks are largely missing.

Finally, this page's evidence base is thin: the 11 entries under-represent an active literature, and much of the most-used machinery appears only as undated library links, so the narrative above is a spine reconstructed from a small sample rather than a census.
