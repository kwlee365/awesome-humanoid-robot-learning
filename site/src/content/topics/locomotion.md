## Definition and scope

This area covers controllers that keep a legged humanoid upright and moving: walking, running, jumping, stair and terrain traversal, recovery from pushes and falls, and the perception modules that feed those controllers. The scope is narrower than whole-body control in general — the object of study is the gait itself, not manipulation while walking, goal selection, or retargeting for expressive behaviour, which belong to adjacent areas of this collection. The boundary is porous: entries such as [Dribble Master](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dribble-master-learning-agile-humanoid-dribbling-through-legged-locomotion) and [Let Humanoids Hike!](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/let-humanoids-hike-integrative-skill-development-on-complex-trails) use a locomotion policy as the substrate for a task that is not really about walking.

## Why this problem matters

A biped is an underactuated, hybrid, contact-rich system: authority over the floating base comes only through intermittent unilateral contacts, and the feasible set of contact wrenches changes discontinuously with every footfall. That makes locomotion the canonical hard problem of legged robotics and a prerequisite for everything else a humanoid does. It is also where the sim-to-real gap is most punishing, because errors compound over a gait cycle and end in a fall rather than a degraded score. Two consequences follow: progress here has repeatedly been the vehicle for methods that later spread to manipulation and whole-body control, and evaluation — how many stairs, how steep, how strong a push — remains largely demonstration-based, a gap that surveys such as [Deep Reinforcement Learning for Bipedal Locomotion: A Brief Survey](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/deep-reinforcement-learning-for-bipedal-locomotion-a-brief-survey) raise explicitly.

## Historical development

The model-based era made the problem tractable through drastic reduction. The zero-moment point criterion, whose history is recounted by Vukobratović and Borovac in "Zero-Moment Point — Thirty Five Years of Its Life" (International Journal of Humanoid Robotics, 2004; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0219843604000083)) (historical reference, not in the list), reduced dynamic balance to a condition on one ground point lying inside the support polygon. Kajita et al.'s 3D Linear Inverted Pendulum Mode (IROS 2001; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS.2001.973365)) and "Biped Walking Pattern Generation by Using Preview Control of Zero-Moment Point" (ICRA 2003; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ROBOT.2003.1241826)) (both historical references, not in the list) turned that criterion into a constructive pipeline: pick footsteps, generate a ZMP reference, solve a linear preview problem for the centre-of-mass trajectory, track it with inverse kinematics. The assumptions were strong — constant CoM height, flat known ground, a rigid position-controlled robot — but they made walking reproducible and defined the architecture that dominated for two decades: a plan layer producing a reference, a tracking layer executing it.

Relaxing those assumptions drove the next fifteen years. Capture point, from Pratt et al.'s "Capture Point: A Step toward Humanoid Push Recovery" (Humanoids 2006; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICHR.2006.321385)) (historical reference, not in the list), replaced a static balance test with a reachability quantity — where the next step must land to stop — making reactive stepping a first-class control action. Orin, Goswami and Lee's "Centroidal Dynamics of a Humanoid Robot" (Autonomous Robots, 2013; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10514-013-9341-4)) (historical reference, not in the list) gave a reduced model that keeps full momentum rather than a point mass, and Kuindersma et al.'s "Optimization-Based Locomotion Planning, Estimation, and Control Design for the Atlas Humanoid Robot" (Autonomous Robots, 2016; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10514-015-9479-3)) (historical reference, not in the list) showed the paradigm's mature form: contact-constrained trajectory optimisation above a real-time whole-body QP mapping task objectives to torques under friction and actuation limits. In parallel, torque-controllable quadrupeds made receding-horizon control cheap enough to run at gait frequency — Di Carlo et al.'s convex MPC on MIT Cheetah 3 (IROS 2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS.2018.8594448)) (historical reference, not in the list) is the reference point — while the ATRIAS and Cassie line of spring-legged bipeds supplied the hardware on which later learning results were demonstrated. The limits were structural: models were only as good as their identified parameters, unmodelled compliance broke the contact schedule, and every new behaviour required new engineering.

Learning entered first as a way to tune, not to replace: Stulp et al.'s "Reinforcement Learning of Full-Body Humanoid Motor Skills" (Humanoids 2010; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICHR.2010.5686320)) (historical reference, not in the list) optimised parameters of an existing controller. The displacement came from quadrupeds, where three ingredients arrived together: accurate actuator models, domain randomisation, and enough simulated experience. Hwangbo et al., "Learning Agile and Dynamic Motor Skills for Legged Robots" (Science Robotics, 2019; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.aau5872)), Lee et al., "Learning Quadrupedal Locomotion over Challenging Terrain" (2020; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.abc5986)) and Miki et al., "Learning Robust Perceptive Locomotion for Quadrupedal Robots in the Wild" (2022; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.abk2822)) (all historical references, not in the list) established the recipe humanoid work still uses: train a privileged teacher, distil into a proprioception-or-vision student, and let a latent adaptation variable absorb what cannot be observed. Kumar et al.'s RMA ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04034)) made that adaptation module explicit and online, and Cassie results such as Siekmann et al.'s blind stair traversal ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08328)) and Li et al.'s parameterised policies ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14295)) (historical references, not in the list) carried the recipe across underactuation and narrow feet.

What made this practical was a change in the compute substrate rather than in the algorithms. GPU-parallel simulation — Isaac Gym ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10470)) and Rudin et al.'s massively parallel training ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11978)) (historical references, not in the list) — moved training budgets from days on clusters to minutes on one workstation, which changed what counted as an experiment: reward shaping, curricula and randomisation ranges became things you could search rather than derive. The trend intensifies in the list: [FastTD3](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/fasttd3-simple-fast-and-capable-reinforcement-learning-for-humanoid-control) and its follow-up [Learning Sim-to-Real Humanoid Locomotion in 15 Minutes](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-sim-to-real-humanoid-locomotion-in-15-minutes) report humanoid policies trained on a single consumer GPU in minutes and transferred to Unitree G1 and Booster T1, and frameworks such as [Booster Gym](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/booster-gym-an-end-to-end-rl-framework-for-humanoid-robot-locomotion) package the pipeline itself as the contribution.

Humanoid-specific results followed quickly. [Real-World Humanoid Locomotion with Reinforcement Learning](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/real-world-humanoid-locomotion-with-reinforcement-learning) (later in Science Robotics) replaced the MLP with a causal transformer over observation-action history, and [Humanoid Locomotion as Next Token Prediction](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-locomotion-as-next-token-prediction) recast control as autoregressive sequence modelling over prior policies, model-based controllers, motion capture and video — a data-centric position rather than a control-theoretic one. Perception re-entered through elevation maps and depth rather than raw images: [Learning Humanoid Locomotion with Perceptive Internal Model](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-humanoid-locomotion-with-perceptive-internal-model) keeps a robot-centred elevation map as the interface, [BeamDojo](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/beamdojo-learning-agile-humanoid-locomotion-on-sparse-footholds) adds foothold-aware rewards, and [Humanoid Parkour Learning](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-parkour-learning) reports vision-conditioned jumping and gap crossing without motion references.

The most recent shift, visible across 2025-2026 entries, is that human motion data and the reduced models both came back — as priors inside a learning loop rather than as the control architecture. [Whole-body Humanoid Robot Locomotion with Human Reference](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/whole-body-humanoid-robot-locomotion-with-human-reference), the adversarial-prior lineage from [DeepMimic](https://doi.org/10.1145/3197517.3201311) and [AMP](https://doi.org/10.1145/3450626.3459670) (historical references, not in the list), and work such as [StyleLoco](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/styleloco-generative-adversarial-distillation-for-natural-humanoid-robot-locomot) and [Natural Humanoid Robot Locomotion with Generative Motion Prior](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/natural-humanoid-robot-locomotion-with-generative-motion-prior) treat naturalness as a learned regulariser. [PHUMA](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/phuma-physically-grounded-humanoid-locomotion-dataset) answers the obvious objection — that mocap and video motion is physically inconsistent for a robot — with physics-aware curation and constrained retargeting. Symmetrically, [Reduced-Order Model-Guided RL](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/reduced-order-model-guided-rl-for-demonstration-free-humanoid-locomotion) and [Chasing Stability](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/chasing-stability-humanoid-running-via-control-lyapunov-function-guided-rl) put reduced-order templates and Lyapunov functions back in as reward or constraint terms. My reading is that this is convergence rather than a swing back: classical theory now supplies inductive bias and certificates, while the policy supplies the contact reasoning the theory could never write down.

## Major technical transitions

Assumptions moved from flat, known, rigid ground with an identified rigid-body model to unknown, deformable and sparse terrain with randomised dynamics; [Robust Humanoid Walking on Compliant and Uneven Terrain](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robust-humanoid-walking-on-compliant-and-uneven-terrain-with-deep-rl) attacks compliance directly. Data sources moved from none, to simulation only, to simulation plus curated human motion, to internet video via [VideoMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/videomimic-visual-imitation-enables-contextual-humanoid-control) and [RoboMirror](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/robomirror-understand-before-you-imitate-for-video-to-humanoid-locomotion). Representations moved from CoM and ZMP references to joint-space policies over observation histories, then to explicit terrain representations — heightmaps in [DPL](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dpl-depth-only-perceptive-humanoid-locomotion-via-realistic-depth-synthesis-and-) and [a fused lidar-depth autoencoder](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/a-hybrid-autoencoder-for-robust-heightmap-generation-from-fused-lidar-and-depth-), polygonal maps in [PolygMap](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/polygmap-a-perceptive-locomotion-framework-for-humanoid-robot-stair-climbing), neural maps in [AME-2](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/ame-2-agile-and-generalized-legged-locomotion-via-attention-based-neural-map-enc). Architectures moved from plan-then-track to monolithic policies and partly back to layered designs, as argued in [Architecture Is All You Need](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/architecture-is-all-you-need-diversity-enabled-sweet-spots-for-robust-humanoid-l). Hardware assumptions changed too: [LiPS](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/lips-large-scale-humanoid-robot-rl-with-parallel-series-structures) and [Mechanical Intelligence-Aware Curriculum RL](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/mechanical-intelligence-aware-curriculum-reinforcement-learning-for-humanoids-wi) address parallel actuation that rigid-serial models did not describe.

## Comparison of dominant approaches

| Approach family | Data requirement | Generality | Robustness | Sim-to-real cost | Typical failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Model-based MPC + whole-body QP ([Atlas stack](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10514-015-9479-3), [convex MPC](https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS.2018.8594448)) | None; identified model instead | Behaviours specified by hand; portable given a model | Good near the modelled regime, degrades sharply outside | Low sim cost, high modelling and tuning cost | Model mismatch; broken contact schedule |
| End-to-end RL + domain randomisation ([Booster Gym](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/booster-gym-an-end-to-end-rl-framework-for-humanoid-robot-locomotion), [FastTD3](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/fasttd3-simple-fast-and-capable-reinforcement-learning-for-humanoid-control)) | Large simulated interaction | One policy per command space; retrain per robot | Strong inside the randomised range | Moderate, falling with GPU simulation | Jittery gaits; failure outside randomisation range |
| Motion-imitation / prior-regularised RL ([StyleLoco](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/styleloco-generative-adversarial-distillation-for-natural-humanoid-robot-locomot), [PHUMA](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/phuma-physically-grounded-humanoid-locomotion-dataset)) | Retargeted, curated human motion | Style transfers; limited to the corpus | Inherits RL robustness; prior can fight recovery | Adds retargeting and embodiment-gap cost | Prior dominates task reward; infeasible references |
| Perceptive hierarchical / hybrid ([HWC-Loco](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/hwc-loco-a-hierarchical-whole-body-control-approach-to-robust-humanoid-locomotio), [layered architecture](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/architecture-is-all-you-need-diversity-enabled-sweet-spots-for-robust-humanoid-l)) | Simulated interaction plus terrain maps | Modular; layers swappable per robot or sensor | Fallback layer holds balance when perception fails | Higher: two interfaces to validate | Interface mismatch; stale elevation maps |
| Safety-filtered / certificate-guided ([SHIELD](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/shield-safety-on-humanoids-via-cbfs-in-expectation-on-learned-dynamics), [predictive filters](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/geometry-aware-predictive-safety-filters-on-humanoids)) | Learned or identified dynamics | Wraps any base policy | Only as valid as the learned model | Extra online computation | Over-conservatism; violated guarantees |

## Representative papers

- [Real-World Humanoid Locomotion with Reinforcement Learning](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/real-world-humanoid-locomotion-with-reinforcement-learning) — history-conditioned transformer policy deployed outdoors; the reference for in-context adaptation on humanoids.
- [Humanoid Locomotion as Next Token Prediction](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-locomotion-as-next-token-prediction) — control recast as sequence modelling over mixed-quality trajectory data.
- [Learning Humanoid Locomotion with Perceptive Internal Model](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-humanoid-locomotion-with-perceptive-internal-model) — elevation map as the interface between perception and policy.
- [BeamDojo](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/beamdojo-learning-agile-humanoid-locomotion-on-sparse-footholds) — reward design for terrain where the support set is sparse rather than rough.
- [Humanoid Parkour Learning](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-parkour-learning) — agile vision-driven skills without motion references.
- [VideoMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/videomimic-visual-imitation-enables-contextual-humanoid-control) — human video plus reconstructed environment becomes context-conditioned control.
- [PHUMA](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/phuma-physically-grounded-humanoid-locomotion-dataset) — physical consistency of the motion corpus as the contribution, not the policy.
- [LocoFormer](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/locoformer-generalist-locomotion-via-long-context-adaptation) — long-context transformer over procedurally generated bodies, arguing for generalist policies.
- [Learning Sim-to-Real Humanoid Locomotion in 15 Minutes](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-sim-to-real-humanoid-locomotion-in-15-minutes) — off-policy RL at scale; how far training cost has fallen.
- [Architecture Is All You Need](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/architecture-is-all-you-need-diversity-enabled-sweet-spots-for-robust-humanoid-l) — layered, multi-timescale structure over monolithic policies on hard terrain.
- [Biomechanical Comparisons Reveal Divergence of Human and Humanoid Gaits](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/biomechanical-comparisons-reveal-divergence-of-human-and-humanoid-gaits) — quantifies how far visually natural robot gaits remain from human kinematics and kinetics.

## Current research frontier

Four threads are visibly active. Cross-embodiment generality: [H-Zero](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/h-zero-cross-humanoid-locomotion-pretraining-enables-few-shot-novel-embodiment-t), [LocoFormer](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/locoformer-generalist-locomotion-via-long-context-adaptation), [Scalable and General Whole-Body Control for Cross-Humanoid Locomotion](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/scalable-and-general-whole-body-control-for-cross-humanoid-locomotion) and [Learning to Get Up Across Morphologies](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-to-get-up-across-morphologies-zero-shot-recovery-with-a-unified-humanoi) target policies that survive a change of robot. Perception toward raw sensing and photorealistic real-to-sim: [GaussGym](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/gaussgym-an-open-source-real-to-sim-framework-for-learning-locomotion-from-pixel) puts Gaussian splatting inside a vectorised simulator, [Now You See That](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/now-you-see-that-learning-end-to-end-humanoid-locomotion-from-raw-pixels) works from pixels, and [VB-Com](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/vb-com-learning-vision-blind-composite-humanoid-locomotion-against-deficient-per) studies degraded perception. Safety as explicit machinery rather than reward terms, via barrier functions and predictive filters. And objectives beyond "do not fall": energy in [ECO](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/eco-energy-constrained-optimization-with-reinforcement-learning-for-humanoid-wal), comfort and compliance in [Preference-Conditioned Multi-Objective RL](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/preference-conditioned-multi-objective-rl-for-integrated-command-tracking-and-fo), speed in [the 100m dash study](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/optimizing-bipedal-locomotion-for-the-100m-dash-with-comparison-to-human-running).

## Open problems and unresolved limitations

Evaluation is the most obvious gap: results are reported as demonstrations on particular terrains and particular robots, so cross-paper comparison is largely impossible, and the biomechanical framing in [Biomechanical Comparisons Reveal Divergence of Human and Humanoid Gaits](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/biomechanical-comparisons-reveal-divergence-of-human-and-humanoid-gaits) is one attempt to add measurable quantities beyond success rate. Energetic efficiency stays under-addressed relative to the passive-dynamic tradition. Guarantees are weak: safety filters built on learned dynamics inherit that model's errors, and there is no accepted way to state what a learned policy will do outside its randomisation range. Perception failure — stale maps, occluded footholds, sensor dropout — is handled by fallback heuristics rather than principled degradation. Cross-embodiment results are recent and reported at the level of episode-duration retention rather than task parity, so how much genuinely transfers is unsettled. Finally, imitation priors and recovery behaviour sit in unresolved tension: a prior that makes nominal walking look human can penalise the non-human motions needed to avoid a fall, and none of the work surveyed here addresses that directly.
