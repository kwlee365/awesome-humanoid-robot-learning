## Definition and scope

This area covers making a humanoid or bimanual robot change the state of objects in the world: grasping, in-hand reorientation, tool use, assembly, folding, handover and other contact-rich tasks. It spans four coupled sub-problems: **what to do** (task and motion planning, language grounding), **how to move** (trajectory generation, impedance and force regulation), **what to learn from** (demonstrations, simulation, human video), and **what to sense with** (multi-camera and egocentric vision, proprioception, tactile skin).

The scope is narrower than "everything a humanoid does with its arms". Tasks whose difficulty comes primarily from balance or base motion belong to loco-manipulation and whole-body control; teleoperation systems as an end in themselves belong to teleoperation. Papers appear in more than one area when they genuinely span both — most demonstration-collection systems below do.

## Why this problem matters

Manipulation is the bottleneck that separates humanoids from wheeled platforms. Locomotion has a well-defined objective, a low-dimensional task space and a simulator that transfers reasonably well; manipulation has none of these. Contact is discontinuous and poorly simulated, the action space is high-dimensional once multi-fingered hands are involved, and there is no internet-scale corpus of robot actions the way there is for text and images. The [In-N-On](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/in-n-on-scaling-egocentric-manipulation-with-in-the-wild-and-on-task-data) authors state the problem plainly: egocentric human video is scalable, robot demonstration data is not.

It also carries the economic argument for anthropomorphic hardware. Human tools, human workspaces and human-scale objects are all manipulation constraints, and studies such as [Humanoids in Hospitals](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoids-in-hospitals-a-technical-study-of-humanoid-surrogates-for-dexterous-me) probe that claim in a specific domain.

## Historical development

**The analytic era: geometry, contact models and quality metrics.** Early dexterous manipulation was a mechanics problem. [Salisbury and Craig's analysis of articulated hands](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/027836498200100102) (IJRR 1982, historical reference, not in the list) framed multi-fingered hands in terms of force control and kinematic conditioning, and [Ferrari and Canny's "Planning optimal grasps"](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~jfc/papers/92/FCicra92.pdf) (ICRA 1992, historical reference, not in the list) supplied the grasp-wrench-space quality measures that dominated the next two decades. The assumptions were strong: object geometry known, contacts modelled as point contacts with friction, perception delegated to a separate pose-estimation module, and evaluation performed in simulation against a scalar quality score. [Bohg et al.'s survey of data-driven grasp synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.2660) (2014, historical reference, not in the list) marks the point where learned object-to-grasp mappings began substituting for exact models.

**Learning enters, first as a grasp classifier.** [Dex-Net 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.09312) (2017, historical reference, not in the list) is the clean example of the hybrid stage: analytic wrench-space metrics were retained, but only as *labels* for millions of synthetic depth images used to train a grasp-quality CNN. The representation changed (depth images instead of meshes) while the assumption of an open-loop, single-shot, parallel-jaw grasp did not.

**End-to-end visuomotor policies dissolve the pipeline.** [Levine et al.'s end-to-end deep visuomotor policies](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-522.html) (JMLR 2016, historical reference, not in the list) removed the perception/planning/control boundary and trained pixels-to-torques networks directly. In parallel, [OpenAI's Dactyl](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00177) (2018, historical reference, not in the list) showed that domain-randomised sim-to-real reinforcement learning could produce in-hand reorientation on a five-fingered hand — at a compute cost, and for a task family narrow enough, that generalisation stayed out of reach. Interpretively, this era fixed the field's two persistent axes: where data comes from, and how much structure the representation keeps.

**Imitation learning and the demonstration-collection arms race.** Schaal's question ["Is imitation learning the route to humanoid robots?"](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/01327-3-is-imitation-learning-the-route-to-humanoid-robots) (1999) anticipated the pivot, but it needed usable teleoperation to become practical. [VR-teleoperated deep imitation learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04615) (2017) and [DexPilot](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03135) (2019, both historical references, not in the list) made hand-arm demonstration collection tractable, and [ALOHA/ACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705) (2023, historical reference, not in the list) showed low-cost bimanual leader-follower rigs plus action chunking could learn fine-grained tasks. Humanoid-specific interfaces followed quickly and changed the *hardware*, not just the software: [Open-TeleVision](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/open-television-teleoperation-with-immersive-active-visual-feedback) gave operators stereoscopic active viewing of the robot's surroundings, [Bunny-VisionPro](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/bunny-visionpro-real-time-bimanual-dexterous-teleoperation-for-imitation-learnin) and [ACE](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/ace-a-cross-platform-visual-exoskeletons-system-for-low-cost-dexterous-teleopera) targeted low-cost cross-platform dexterous control, and [Bimanual Dexterity for Complex Tasks](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/bimanual-dexterity-for-complex-tasks) pushed glove-driven bimanual multi-fingered teleoperation. A distinct thread argued that the *camera itself* should be controlled: [Learning to Look Around](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-to-look-around-enhancing-teleoperation-and-learning-with-a-human-like-a) added an actuated neck, [Learning to Look](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-to-look-seeking-information-for-decision-making-via-policy-factorizatio) factorised information-seeking from acting, and [Vision in Action](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/vision-in-action-learning-active-perception-from-human-demonstrations) learned active perception from demonstrations.

**Diffusion and flow replace regression.** Behaviour cloning with unimodal regression heads averages over inconsistent human demonstrations. [Diffusion Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04137) (RSS 2023, historical reference, not in the list) reframed the policy as a conditional generative model over short action chunks, which handles multimodality and long-horizon smoothness far better. Humanoid work adopted this rapidly: [improved 3D diffusion policies](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/generalizable-humanoid-manipulation-with-improved-3d-diffusion-policies) trained a full-size 25-DoF humanoid on egocentric 3D point-cloud observations and reported skill execution in diverse scenes from single-scene training data. Scale followed: [RDT-1B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07864) and [π0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24164) (2024, historical references, not in the list) established billion-parameter diffusion/flow-matching action experts, and [H-RDT](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/h-rdt-human-manipulation-enhanced-bimanual-robotic-manipulation) added a human-video pretraining stage in front of them.

**Robot-free data collection: hand-held, wearable, and augmented.** The most consequential hardware idea of 2024–2026 is that demonstrations need not involve a robot. [UMI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10329) (2024, historical reference, not in the list) used a hand-held gripper with a wrist camera. [DexCap](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dexcap-scalable-and-portable-mocap-data-collection-system-for-dexterous-manipula) extended this to fingers with SLAM plus electromagnetic tracking and paired point clouds, and [ARMADA](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/armada-augmented-reality-for-robot-manipulation-and-robot-free-data-acquisition) used augmented reality to make robot-free acquisition feasible. [DexUMI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dexumi-using-human-hand-as-the-universal-manipulation-interface-for-dexterous-ma) closed the loop on both gaps at once: a wearable exoskeleton constrains human motion to what the robot hand can execute, and robot-hand *inpainting* rewrites the human hand out of the video. [ActiveUMI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/activeumi-robotic-manipulation-with-active-perception-from-robot-free-human-demo) added head motion so the resulting policies also learn where to look, [DexHub and DART](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dexhub-and-dart-towards-internet-scale-robot-data-collection) argued for a shared collection substrate, and [DexterCap](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dextercap-an-affordable-and-automated-system-for-capturing-dexterous-hand-object) and [HumDex](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humdex-humanoid-dexterous-manipulation-made-easy) continued the cost-reduction trajectory into 2026.

**Egocentric human video as a first-class data source.** [Ego4D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07058) (CVPR 2022, historical reference, not in the list) provided thousands of hours of egocentric footage but no native hand-pose labels, and [DexMV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05877) (ECCV 2022, historical reference, not in the list) showed video-to-simulation retargeting for dexterous hands. The humanoid literature then made human data structurally equal to robot data rather than a pretraining afterthought. [OKAMI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/okami-teaching-humanoid-robots-manipulation-skills-through-single-video-imitatio) derived a humanoid policy from a single RGB-D video via object-aware retargeting; [EgoMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egomimic-scaling-imitation-learning-via-egocentric-video) captured Aria-glasses data and co-trained a unified policy, reporting that an extra hour of hand data was worth more than an extra hour of robot data; [Humanoid Policy ~ Human Policy](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-policy-human-policy) built a unified human-robot state-action space; and [EgoDex](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egodex-learning-dexterous-manipulation-from-large-scale-egocentric-video) collected 829 hours of Vision Pro video with paired 3D finger tracking across 194 tasks. The residual problems — appearance mismatch, viewpoint sensitivity — spawned their own methods: [Masquerade](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/masquerade-learning-from-in-the-wild-human-videos-using-data-editing) edits in-the-wild video, [EgoDemoGen](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egodemogen-novel-egocentric-demonstration-generation-enables-viewpoint-robust-ma) synthesises novel egocentric viewpoints, and [Dexterity from Smart Lenses](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dexterity-from-smart-lenses-multi-fingered-robot-manipulation-with-in-the-wild-h) targets in-the-wild capture with consumer eyewear.

**Vision-language-action models and generative world models.** [RT-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15818), [Open X-Embodiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08864) and [OpenVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09246) (2023–2024, historical references, not in the list) established that web-pretrained vision-language backbones with action heads generalise better than task-specific policies. [GR00T N1](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/gr00t-n1-an-open-foundation-model-for-generalist-humanoid-robots) applied this to humanoids with a dual-system design — a vision-language reasoning module plus a diffusion-transformer action module — trained on a mixture of robot trajectories, human video and synthetic data. Human-video-pretrained VLAs followed with [EgoVLA](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egovla-learning-vision-language-action-models-from-egocentric-human-videos) and [Being-H0](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/being-h0-vision-language-action-pretraining-from-large-scale-human-videos), the latter introducing part-level hand-motion tokenisation. A parallel branch treats generated video as data or as a simulator: [Unified Video Action Model](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/unified-video-action-model), [DreamGen](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dreamgen-unlocking-generalization-in-robot-learning-through-neural-trajectories) (synthetic "neural trajectories" with pseudo-actions recovered by latent-action or inverse-dynamics models), [DreamZero](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dreamzero-world-action-models-are-zero-shot-policies), and [DreamDojo](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dreamdojo-a-generalist-robot-world-model-from-large-scale-human-videos), a world model trained on 44,000 hours of egocentric human video using continuous latent actions as proxy labels.

**What did not go away.** Analytic methods were absorbed rather than replaced. GPU-parallel planners in the [cuRobo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17274) lineage (ICRA 2023, historical reference, not in the list) reappear as [cuRoboV2](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/curobov2-dynamics-aware-motion-generation-with-depth-fused-distance-fields-for-h) with depth-fused distance fields for high-DoF robots, [Lightning Grasp](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/lightning-grasp-high-performance-procedural-grasp-synthesis-with-contact-fields) revives procedural grasp synthesis via contact fields, and safety filters such as [Dexterous Safe Control](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dexterous-safe-control-for-humanoids-in-cluttered-environments-via-projected-saf) sit around learned policies. Similarly, tactile sensing — long promised since [GelSight](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/17/12/2762) (Sensors 2017, historical reference, not in the list) — is now entering learned pipelines through [visuotactile bimanual skills](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/learning-visuotactile-skills-with-two-multifingered-hands), a [humanoid visual-tactile-action dataset](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/a-humanoid-visual-tactile-action-dataset-for-contact-rich-manipulation), and [visual-tactile pretraining with online multitask learning](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/visual-tactile-pretraining-and-online-multitask-learning-for-humanlike-manipulat) in *Science Robotics*.

## Major technical transitions

- **Object models to pixels.** Known-geometry grasp planning gave way to depth- and RGB-conditioned learning, then to 3D point-cloud policies and egocentric multi-view inputs.
- **Regression to generative action modelling.** Deterministic behaviour cloning was superseded by diffusion and flow-matching action chunks that represent multimodal demonstration distributions.
- **Robot-collected to human-collected data.** Leader-follower teleoperation was joined by hand-held, wearable and AR capture rigs, and then by passively recorded egocentric video.
- **Pretraining target shifted from robot corpora to human corpora.** Cross-embodiment robot datasets are increasingly complemented — sometimes replaced — by human hand-motion pretraining.
- **Static cameras to active perception.** Actuated necks, head-tracked VR and learned gaze policies made "where to look" part of the action space.
- **Real data to co-trained and generated data.** Simulation co-training, neural trajectories and world models became accepted data sources rather than curiosities.
- **Vision-only to vision-language-tactile.** Language conditioning arrived through VLA backbones; tactile and impedance/force signals are the current frontier of the observation and action interface.

## Representative papers

- [DexCap](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dexcap-scalable-and-portable-mocap-data-collection-system-for-dexterous-manipula) — portable SLAM plus electromagnetic finger mocap with paired point clouds, and an accompanying imitation algorithm.
- [Open-TeleVision](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/open-television-teleoperation-with-immersive-active-visual-feedback) — immersive stereoscopic teleoperation validated by training policies on four long-horizon tasks across two humanoids.
- [OKAMI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/okami-teaching-humanoid-robots-manipulation-skills-through-single-video-imitatio) — single RGB-D video to humanoid execution through object-aware retargeting.
- [EgoMimic](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egomimic-scaling-imitation-learning-via-egocentric-video) — Aria-glasses capture plus a kinematically matched manipulator, co-training human and robot data in one policy.
- [Generalizable Humanoid Manipulation with Improved 3D Diffusion Policies](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/generalizable-humanoid-manipulation-with-improved-3d-diffusion-policies) — 3D point-cloud diffusion policy deployed on a 25-DoF humanoid with onboard sensing.
- [Sim-to-Real RL for Vision-Based Dexterous Manipulation on Humanoids](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/sim-to-real-reinforcement-learning-for-vision-based-dexterous-manipulation-on-hu) — automated real-to-sim tuning and policy distillation for bimanual multi-fingered tasks without demonstrations.
- [GR00T N1](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/gr00t-n1-an-open-foundation-model-for-generalist-humanoid-robots) — open humanoid foundation model pairing a vision-language module with a diffusion-transformer action module.
- [DexUMI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dexumi-using-human-hand-as-the-universal-manipulation-interface-for-dexterous-ma) — wearable exoskeletons for two robot hands plus robot-hand video inpainting to remove the visual gap.
- [EgoDex](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egodex-learning-dexterous-manipulation-from-large-scale-egocentric-video) — 829 hours of Vision Pro egocentric video with joint-level hand tracking across 194 tabletop tasks.
- [H-RDT](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/h-rdt-human-manipulation-enhanced-bimanual-robotic-manipulation) — 2B-parameter flow-matching diffusion transformer pretrained on human video, then cross-embodiment fine-tuned.
- [DreamDojo](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dreamdojo-a-generalist-robot-world-model-from-large-scale-human-videos) — real-time world model from 44,000 hours of human video, used for teleoperation, policy evaluation and planning.

## Current research frontier

Four threads dominate the 2025–2026 entries. First, **human video at scale as the pretraining substrate**, spanning capture ([DexterCap](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dextercap-an-affordable-and-automated-system-for-capturing-dexterous-hand-object), [Dexterity from Smart Lenses](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dexterity-from-smart-lenses-multi-fingered-robot-manipulation-with-in-the-wild-h)) and policy learning ([EgoMI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egomi-learning-active-vision-and-whole-body-manipulation-from-egocentric-human-d), [MimicDroid](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/mimicdroid-in-context-learning-for-humanoid-robot-manipulation-from-human-play-v)). Second, **generated data**: [DreamGen](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dreamgen-unlocking-generalization-in-robot-learning-through-neural-trajectories), [EgoDemoGen](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/egodemogen-novel-egocentric-demonstration-generation-enables-viewpoint-robust-ma), [MobileH2R](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/mobileh2r-learning-generalizable-human-to-mobile-robot-handover-exclusively-from), and systematic studies of [sim-and-real co-training](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/sim-and-real-co-training-a-simple-recipe-for-vision-based-robotic-manipulation) and [co-training data modalities](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/a-systematic-study-of-data-modalities-and-strategies-for-co-training-large-behav). Third, **language and reasoning above control**: [hierarchical vision-language planning](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/hierarchical-vision-language-planning-for-multi-step-humanoid-manipulation), [proprioception-aware embodied planning](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/towards-proprioception-aware-embodied-planning-for-dual-arm-humanoid-robots), VLM-mediated compliance in [HumanoidVLM](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoidvlm-vision-language-guided-impedance-control-for-contact-rich-humanoid-m) and [SafeHumanoid](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/safehumanoid-vlm-rag-driven-control-of-upper-body-impedance-for-humanoid-robot), and attempts to bolt off-the-shelf VLMs onto bodies ([Endowing GPT-4 with a Humanoid Body](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/endowing-gpt-4-with-a-humanoid-body-building-the-bridge-between-off-the-shelf-vl)). Fourth, **evaluation infrastructure** such as [Humanoid Everyday](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/humanoid-everyday-a-comprehensive-robotic-dataset-for-open-world-humanoid-manipu) and [Genie Sim 3.0](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/genie-sim-3-0-a-high-fidelity-comprehensive-simulation-platform-for-humanoid-rob), which exist because per-paper task suites made cross-paper comparison nearly impossible.

## Open problems and unresolved limitations

- **Force and contact are still under-represented.** Most scalable data sources record kinematics only. Wearable rigs capture where fingers went, not how hard they pressed; approaches such as [DexUMI](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/dexumi-using-human-hand-as-the-universal-manipulation-interface-for-dexterous-ma) add haptic feedback for the operator, but tactile-labelled datasets like the [visual-tactile-action dataset](/awesome-humanoid-robot-learning/papers/a-humanoid-visual-tactile-action-dataset-for-contact-rich-manipulation) remain small relative to video corpora.
- **The embodiment gap is patched, not solved.** Inpainting, exoskeleton constraints and unified state-action spaces each address one facet; no single formulation handles morphology, dynamics, appearance and sensing mismatch together.
- **Evaluation is not comparable across papers.** Success rates are reported on bespoke task sets with varying numbers of rollouts, and there is no widely adopted humanoid manipulation benchmark with standardised objects, scenes and protocols.
- **Precision versus semantics.** Large VLAs generalise across instructions and scenes but tend to lose the millimetre-scale accuracy that insertion, threading and tool-use tasks demand; residual RL fine-tuning is one proposed remedy but adds real-robot interaction cost.
- **Safety and compliance under uncertainty.** Impedance-aware and safe-set approaches are still bolted on around learned policies rather than trained jointly, so guarantees degrade exactly where learned behaviour is least reliable.
- **Hand hardware remains the limiter.** Multi-fingered hands with durable tactile skin, sufficient force output and repeatable calibration are rare and expensive, which constrains reproduction of the software results above.
